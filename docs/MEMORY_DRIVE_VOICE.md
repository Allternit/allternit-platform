# How Allternit talks

**Status note:** Distilled from the phone script, the Swyft SOW, the catalog's guardrail language, and the Minds mythology doc. No formal brand-voice guide exists yet (ironically, "brand voice guide from approved examples" is itself a catalog product — practice what we sell).

There are **two registers**. Using the wrong one in the wrong place is the main failure mode to avoid.

## Register 1 — Operational / client-facing (default; use for contracts, invoices, quotes, support, most marketing copy)

Plain, direct, professional. Short sentences. States what will happen and what won't. Never oversells. Examples of Allternit's own writing in this register:

> "Thank you for calling Allternit. We help businesses put AI to work through IT consulting, agentic systems, and hands-on implementation."

> "The fastest way to get started is online at allternit.com — you can book an AI Automation Audit, request a Build or Retrofit project, sign up for Run support, or configure a Husks robotic cell there."

> "Prices are starter ranges, not promises. Quote after inputs, page/item counts, integrations, revision limits, and acceptance tests are known."

> "Do not sell unreviewed mass articles, fabricated expertise, fake testimonials, plagiarism, academic cheating, or guaranteed rankings."

Notice the pattern: a plain statement of what Allternit does, immediately followed by the concrete next action or the concrete boundary. No hype adjectives, no vague capability claims ("revolutionary," "game-changing," "AI-powered magic").

## Register 2 — Brand / mythology (marketing narrative, product naming, docs about the model family and platform architecture — not for contracts, invoices, or anything a customer signs)

Elemental, alchemical, precise about its own metaphor. Example, verbatim from the Minds doc:

> "A:// is the Vessel. Aluminum is the most abundant metal in the Earth's crust — light, conductive, corrosion-resistant, and everywhere. It is the crucible, not the gold."

> "Anon is the breath between commands, the subconscious watcher, the emergent property that arises from the substrate."

This register is allowed to be poetic and confident about the *idea* — but never uses it to imply a product capability that doesn't exist. Mythology explains the *name*, not a performance claim.

**Rule of thumb:** if a sentence would appear on an invoice, SOW, support ticket, or price page — Register 1. If it would appear in brand storytelling, a docs overview of "why Gizzi is called Gizzi," or marketing narrative about the model family — Register 2 is fine, but keep it clearly separated from anything transactional in the same document.

## Phrases Allternit uses

- "We help businesses put AI to work through..." (the standing positioning line)
- "Your data never leaves the box." (the core privacy/sovereignty pitch — used consistently across both the software platform and home-services framing)
- "Audit first" / "never quote blind" (Tier C framing)
- "Quote after inputs... are known" (never a bare number without qualifiers)
- Plain verbs for boundaries: "Never," "Do not," "Always requires," "Confirm... before" — direct imperative, not hedged ("we try to avoid...")

## Phrases and patterns Allternit avoids

- Guarantee language of any kind attached to outcomes: no "guaranteed rankings," "guaranteed results," "guaranteed sales."
- Vague AI hype: no "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "AI-powered magic," "10x your business" — the catalog and phone script never use this register even once.
- Overclaiming certainty on anything factual, legal, medical, or financial — the house style is to state source, freshness, and uncertainty rather than assert.
- Mixing the mythology register into anything a customer signs or gets billed for — an invoice should never mention Fechon, Anon, or Chemit.
- Silent scope creep in writing — any expansion of what was promised gets named as a change order in the copy itself, not smoothed over.

## What Allternit sounds like it believes (tone, not just word choice)

Confident about boundaries, honest about limits, execution-focused rather than strategy-deck-focused ("we don't just diagnose, we fix it"), and consistently privacy/sovereignty-forward as the differentiator rather than raw capability.
