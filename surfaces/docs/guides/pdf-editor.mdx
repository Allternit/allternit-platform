A PDF artifact opens in the PDF editor when you have edit access. You edit the
PDF itself, the way you would in Acrobat: the file you download afterwards is
an ordinary PDF that opens anywhere. Every change saves as a new version of the
artifact, so you can always go back.

To get a PDF in, open **Artifacts**, press **+** and choose **Upload → PDF** (up to 15 MB).
The PDF opens in the editor. Without edit access, a PDF opens read-only.

## Change existing text

1. Click a line of text to select it.
2. Click it again, double-click it, or press **Enter** to type.
3. Press **Enter** or click outside the line to apply the change. **Esc** cancels.

The edit keeps the PDF's own font and size, and only the words you change are
rewritten. Text before and after your edit stays exactly where it was, and
anything later on the same line moves over by the width your edit added or
removed, so nothing overlaps.

PDFs often include only the letters a document used from each font. When you
type a letter the PDF's copy of the font does not have, the editor looks for
the same font in this order:

1. Another copy of the same font inside the PDF that has the letter.
2. The same family and weight from the open Google Fonts library.
3. A font drawn to the same letter widths, for common system fonts (Arimo for
   Arial and Helvetica, Tinos for Times, Cousine for Courier, Carlito for
   Calibri, Caladea for Cambria, Gelasio for Georgia, Inter for Apple's system
   font). A message names the stand-in, with **Undo**.
4. If none of those is available, the closest standard font. A message says so,
   with **Undo**. The editor never swaps a font without telling you.

While a line is selected, the bar above the page changes its size, colour,
bold, italic or font. Choosing **Helvetica**, **Times** or **Courier** there
sets the line in that font on purpose.

## Move, resize, duplicate and delete

With the Edit tool, point at a page and a faint box shows around every text line, image and shape you can edit. The one under the pointer gets a stronger outline. Click one to select it. Then:

- **Drag** it to move it.
- **Drag a corner** to resize it. Text and images keep their proportions.
- **Arrow keys** nudge it 1 point; hold **Shift** for 10 points.
- **Cmd+D** or the duplicate button copies it, a little down and to the right, and selects the copy. Text copies keep the PDF's own font.
- **Delete** or the trash button removes it.

To work on several things at once:

- **Shift**-click adds an item to the selection, or takes it out.
- Drag across empty space on the page to draw a box. Everything it touches is selected.
- **Cmd+A**, or **Select all** in the bar under the tools, selects everything on the page in view.

The selected items then move, resize, nudge, duplicate and delete together. Scanned lines are part of the picture, so they are edited one at a time and are left out of a multiple selection.

## Zoom

Use the zoom buttons or **Cmd+=** and **Cmd+-**, or pinch on a trackpad or hold **Cmd** (Ctrl on Windows) and scroll. Zoom goes from 30% to 400%, and pinching keeps the spot under your fingers in place. The percentage button fits the page to the width again. On a phone, pinch with two fingers.

## Add to the page

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| Text | Click empty space and type. **Shift+Enter** starts a new line. |
| Image | Choose a picture, then click where it goes. You can also drag image files from your computer onto a page: each lands where you drop it. |
| Sign | Draw, type or upload a signature, then click where it goes. |
| Draw | Draw freehand. Pick a colour and line width in the bar. |
| Shape | Drag a rectangle, ellipse or line. |
| Highlight | Drag across text. |
| Comment | Click to pin a comment. Click a comment to edit or delete it. |
| Whiteout | Drag to cover an area with white. |
| Redact | Drag over content to delete the text and images under the box from the file, then paint the box black. |

A signature placed with **Sign** is a picture of your signature on the page.
It is not a certified digital signature.

### Drag files onto the page

Drag an image from your computer onto a page and it is placed where you drop it, on top of whatever is there. Drag a PDF onto a page and its pages go in after that page. While you drag over the pages, the drop screen says **Drop to place on the page**. Drop files anywhere else, such as on the chat, to attach them as usual.

### Whiteout and replace

A whiteout stays on the page under anything you put on it:

- Click a whiteout with **Text** and type. If the whiteout hides a line, your text starts where that line was, in its size and colour, so the new value takes the old one's place.
- Put an image, a signature or a shape on a whiteout and it shows on top.
- Drag text or an image onto a whiteout, an image or a filled box, and it comes to the front.
- Retype part of a line that a whiteout partly covers, and the new text shows on top. The rest of the line stays as it was, so words you covered stay hidden.

Text hidden under a whiteout can't be selected or clicked by accident. Click the whiteout itself to move or delete it, and **Undo** brings back what was there before.

## Scanned PDFs

Some PDFs are pictures of paper: a scan, or a form saved as images. The editor
makes them editable:

1. When you open one, the editor reads each scanned page on your device (text
   recognition, nothing is uploaded). The hint bar shows **Reading scanned
   page 2 so its text can be edited (2 of 10)…**. A page takes a few seconds,
   and the page you're on is read first.
2. The page looks exactly the same, but its lines can now be clicked, edited
   and searched, like any other PDF. The text layer is saved in the file, so
   the next time it opens straight away.
3. Click a line, click again and type. The editor measures the type on that
   line from the scan — font family and weight, size, width, baseline, word
   spacing, stroke thickness and ink colour — and matches it to an open font.
   The style bar shows the match (for example **Source Sans**). When you
   press **Enter**, the old line is covered with the paper colour and your
   text is set in the matched font in the same place.

Only the lines you change are re-typeset; every other line stays the original
scan. **Undo** puts the scanned line back. Scanned lines can't be dragged
(they are part of the picture); **Delete** covers the line.

If no text can be read on a page (a photo, or handwriting), the hint bar says
so. Use **Text** to type on it, **Whiteout** to cover something first, **Sign**
for signature lines and **Draw** to tick a box. Clicking the page in **Edit**
mode doesn't select the scan itself, so a stray drag can't move the whole page.

### How it works (developers)

- OCR: Tesseract.js 7 (Apache-2.0) with the English LSTM model, served from
  `/ocr/` by `vite-plugins/ocr-engine.ts`; no CDN. Each recognised chunk
  becomes an invisible text object stretched over its ink
  (`PdfEngine.addScanText`).
- Font match: `src/lib/pdf/editor/scan-font.ts` compares the line's pixels,
  word by word, with the same text in 36 open families (Fontsource), then
  nearby weights of the best one.
- Edit: `PdfEngine.setScanRunText` covers the old ink and sets the text one
  word per object at the scan's word gap, with extra stroke when the scan's
  type is heavier.

## Agent tools

When a PDF is open beside a chat (for example one you attached), the agent
edits it through the editor: `read_page`, `replace_text`, `set_line`,
`add_text`, `fill_field`, `highlight`, `delete_pages` and `rotate_pages`.
Its edits follow the same font rules as yours and show on the page as it
makes them. See [Attachments](/guides/attachments).

## Fill forms

Fields in a fillable PDF become inputs on the page: type in text fields, tick
checkboxes and pick from lists. The values are saved in the PDF's form, so
other PDF apps show them too.

## Pages

**Pages** opens the page panel. Drag thumbnails to reorder them. Click a page
to select it; **Cmd**-click or **Shift**-click selects more than one. The
buttons work on the selected pages, or on the page in view when none are selected:

- **Rotate** turns pages 90°.
- **Duplicate** copies them.
- **Blank** inserts an empty page after them.
- **Insert PDF** adds the pages of another PDF after them. The same button (a page with a plus) is also in the top toolbar, so it works with the panel closed, which is the default on a phone.
- **Extract** downloads them as a new PDF.
- **Delete** removes them. A PDF keeps at least one page.

## Find, undo and download

- **Cmd+F** finds text. **Enter** goes to the next match and **Shift+Enter** to the previous one.
- **Cmd+Z** undoes and **Cmd+Shift+Z** redoes, across every kind of change, including page changes, duplicates and changes to several items at once.
- The download button saves the current PDF, edits included.

## On a phone

On ai.allternit.com on a phone and in the Allternit app (m.allternit.com) the
tools sit in a bar at the bottom of the screen and **Pages** opens full screen.
Tap text to select it and tap again to type. Drag with one finger to move or
resize what is selected, and pinch with two fingers to zoom. When something is
selected, the boxes around everything else on that page show too. **Select all**
in the bar above the tools selects the whole page.

## Advanced tools

The toolbox button opens the PDF in advanced tools for the PDF security
settings, the accessibility checker and the rest of an Acrobat-style
workbench. Save there to keep what you change; **Back to editor** reopens this
editor on the saved PDF.

## How it works

The editor runs [PDFium](https://pdfium.googlesource.com/pdfium/), the PDF
engine in Chrome, compiled to WebAssembly (the `@embedpdf/pdfium` build), in a
web worker on your device. Your PDF is not sent anywhere to be edited. The
editor only goes online to download a font from the open font library
(cdn.jsdelivr.net) when the PDF's own font is missing a letter you typed.

| Part | Source |
|---|---|
| Engine (open, edit, render, save, undo) | `allternit-ai/src/lib/pdf/editor/engine.ts` |
| Worker and its client | `src/lib/pdf/editor/worker.ts`, `client.ts` |
| Font lookup (font library, metric twins, WOFF → TTF) | `src/lib/pdf/editor/fonts.ts`, `woff.ts` |
| Editor UI | `src/components/pdf-editor/` |
| Registered as the `pdf` kind's editor | `src/lib/artifacts/kinds.ts` |
| Dev preview | `preview-pdf-editor.html` in a `vite` dev server |

Text edits change the page's text objects and then rewrite the page content.
Undo restores a saved copy of the document from before each change. Desktop's
content security policy includes `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` so the engine can compile.
