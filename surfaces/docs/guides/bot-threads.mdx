A bot can have several threads. Each thread tracks an objective, success criteria, checklist, status, dependencies, summary, and the session currently executing it. The rail and project view read the same server records, including work started by the coordinator rather than the current browser window.

## Your bot's stage

A bot's chat and its threads open on the bot itself, big, on a soft wash of the bot's own color, with its conversation beside it. Choosing a bot in the Bots rail (or finishing main bot setup) lands here, on its main chat. The bot floats and leans toward your pointer. Under it:

- **What it made.** A montage that plays through the files and results from this thread and the steps it finished, with the first lines of each document. Click a frame to open it in the thread pane.
- **Up next.** The bot's next routine.

Beside it is the conversation itself: its greeting and starter suggestions for a new chat, its replies, and approvals and questions to answer. The newest messages always show, however old.

- **Needs you** lists the bot's other threads that are waiting on you. Tap one to open it.
- **Since you were last here** says which of its threads moved and which routines ran while you were away.
- **What I made** plays real previews of what the bot saved, across all its threads (documents, sheets, slides, pages, images), then what this chat made and the steps it finished.
- **Plan**: after **Plan it first**, the plan the bot wrote waits in a card. **Approve plan** tells it to go ahead and leaves plan mode. **Change it** lets you reply instead.
- While it works with no checklist, its live steps show (searching, reading, writing). While the bot has an unfinished checklist, its steps show above the conversation with the current one marked. While it works on its computer, that computer shows live in the bot's place, with **Stop** and **Take over**.

**When you send a message, start a task or scroll back through the conversation, the bot flies up into the thread top** and becomes the avatar beside its name and the chat's name. The conversation then takes the screen. **Tap that avatar to bring the bot back out.** The gear in the thread top opens the bot's **Settings** in the [Bot tab](#the-deck-above-the-composer); **Back** returns to the bot screen.

**Tap the bot to talk** (voice starts in this chat). **Drop a file or link on the bot** to hand it work, or use **choose a file** (on phones, **Hand it a file**). The bot asks how to start:

- **Plan it first** sends the work in plan mode. The bot lays out the steps and waits for your go-ahead, which arrives as an approval in the conversation.
- **Just do it** sends it as normal work.
- **Ask me questions** asks the bot to ask what it needs before it starts.

You can add a note before you pick. Files travel the same way as [composer attachments](/guides/attachments): documents as text, images as images, and video, audio and other files as links to your Allternit Cloud storage.

Channel threads, vendor look-pack threads and chats embedded in a project panel show the conversation only. On phones (the `ai.allternit.com` phone layout and the `m.allternit.com` PWA) and in narrow windows, the conversation runs under the bot, and the bot docks the same way. If your system is set to reduce motion, the bot moves to the thread top without flying and its cards stay still.

## The deck above the composer

A bot's chat has four tabs in the tray above the composer. The composer itself, its Agent mode and Plugins row, the model and voice stay the same on every tab.

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **The open thread** (its name, such as **Main chat**) | The chat: the bot beside its conversation, as described above. |
| **Threads** | Every thread with this bot. **Main chat** is pinned on top; the rest are grouped Needs you, Working, Queued and Idle, with search, a project filter, **+ New thread** and **Incognito**. Each row shows how many of its tasks are done. Picking a thread opens it here, in place of the one you were in. |
| **Tasks** | The work inside the open thread: the bot's plan with the current step marked, and the work it **handed off** to subagents nested under the step that was running, as subtasks. **Up next** lists threads waiting their turn and the bot's next routine runs. Main chat usually has no tasks of its own, so it lists the threads that do. |
| **Bot** | The bot detail view: its header (photo, name, status, where it runs, **Open phone**, **Edit bot**) and **Artifacts**, **Routines**, **Memory** and **Settings**. |

A thread is its own chat with the bot. Tasks are the steps the bot is doing inside one thread, with subtasks; they are not separate threads. Sending a message from any tab returns you to the open thread so you see the reply.

The computer and the thread panel stay in the thread top's icons, not in the deck.

**The bot's colour.** A bot's colour is the one picked for it; if none was picked, the stage reads it from the bot's own avatar art (Gem's pink, Ini's green). The stage's wash and your own message bubbles wear that colour. Greyscale art, such as the A:// mark, keeps the neutral look.

**What the bot is doing.** The pill in the thread top has a status line under the bot's name. While the bot works it shows **Searching the web**, **Reading**, **Writing**, **Running a command**, **Using its computer**, **Handing off work** or **Thinking**. **Needs you** (in amber) means the thread, or an approval or question in this chat, waits on you. Otherwise it shows the thread's own status, such as **Idle** or **Done**, or **Ready**.

**Tasks above the composer.** A card above the composer reads **Tasks in this thread · 2 of 4**. It lists the thread's plan, or, with no plan, the steps the bot takes this turn. Work the bot handed to other bots shows under the running step. In the main chat with no plan, it lists the bot's open threads that have tasks (**Tasks in 3 threads**); tap one to open it. Tap the header to open or fold the list (open on desktop, folded on phones). **All tasks** opens the Tasks tab. While the stage shows its own steps, the card stays hidden.

**Needs you on phones.** On the `ai.allternit.com` phone layout and the `m.allternit.com` PWA, a **Needs you** pill above the composer opens a sheet with the bot's pending approvals and the threads waiting on you, so you can answer without scrolling back.

On phones (the `ai.allternit.com` phone layout and the `m.allternit.com` PWA) the tabs drop their icons and a long thread name is shortened. Channel threads keep their own composer and have no deck.

## Standing and task threads

| Kind | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `standing` | A bot's continuing conversation or a routine's home. It stays open rather than resolving as a finished task. |
| `task` | A specific objective that can resolve as done or failed. |

Every bot has one always-on **main chat** (its standing conversation). It knows the status of the bot's other threads but not their full context. Choosing a bot in the Bots rail opens its main chat on [its stage](#your-bots-stage); its threads are on its [bot screen](/surfaces/bots#bot-screen) (the avatar in the thread top, or **Back**). You can also create a new thread for a separate task. **Incognito** threads are private: they are left out of the bot's thread list and the main chat's view of its work. In a group conversation, a bot mention can start a task thread; the inline card opens that work and exposes its running state.

Open threads with no activity for 7 days are marked **Stale** automatically and fold to the bottom of the list. A thread waiting on you is never marked stale.

## Switching threads

In a thread, click the thread title (with the small arrow) to open the switcher. It lists, in order:

1. The bot's **Main chat**.
2. The bot's other threads, grouped Needs you, Working, Queued and Idle, then a folded **Stale** group.
3. **Same project · other bots**, when the thread belongs to a project.

Type to filter by title. Use the arrow keys and Enter, or click; **Esc** closes. Picking a thread opens its session in place. The footer has **+ New thread**, **+ Incognito** and a link to all of the bot's threads on its bot screen. On a phone the switcher opens as a bottom sheet.

## Status and progress

| Server status | Panel group |
| --- | --- |
| `needs_you`, `blocked`, `review` | Waiting on you |
| `working`, `planning` | Working |
| `queued` | Queued |
| `idle`, `paused` | Idle |
| `done`, `failed` | Resolved |

Checklist progress is reported as completed steps out of total steps. Review means the result needs attention; it is not a claim that the task has been approved. Use Stop on an active turn to interrupt execution, including a turn started by the server. Stopping a turn and resolving a task are separate actions.

## Bots handing work to other bots

A bot's own chat (its main chat or one of its threads) can hand a task to another of **your** bots with the `message_agent` tool. This is how your [main bot](/surfaces/bots#your-main-bot) routes work to the bot that owns it.

- **Who exists.** `message_agent` with target `list` returns your other bots with their title, tagline and description, so the bot can pick the owner. Targets resolve by name, `@handle` or id, case-insensitive; an unknown or ambiguous name returns the choices instead of guessing.
- **Where the work goes.** The target gets a task thread (created by `bot`) linked under the caller's thread (`parentThreadId`) and in the same project, so it shows in the target bot's thread list and on the project page like any other thread. A follow-up from the same chat to the same bot continues that thread while it is open; a second hand-off while it is still working is refused with "still working".
- **What comes back.** The target runs the task at once. If it replies within the wait window (90 seconds by default), the reply is the tool's result. Longer work returns a "started" handle with the thread id, and the result is posted into the caller's chat when it lands. The thread moves to **Review** with the reply as its summary, or to **Waiting on you** with the reason if the turn fails.
- **Ownership.** The calling bot comes from the chat's session, never from the model's input, and the target must belong to the same account. Group rooms, regular chats and subagents do not get the tool.
- **Terminal bots** (made with `gizzi agents bot`, kept in `~/.gizzi/bots`) keep the inbox model: the message lands in the target's inbox and its reply arrives later as a background message.

In the chat's process block the call reads **Handed to Scout**, with a **receipt chip** beside it that shows how far the hand-off got: **Delivered**, then **Read** (the target started its turn), then **Answered**. A failed turn reads **Failed** with the reason. On a phone the chip wraps under the line.

### Receipts, the loop guard and retries

- **Receipts.** Every hand-off is recorded as a hop with a state: `stored → delivered → read → answered`, or `failed` / `held` with a reason. The tool result carries it as `receipt`.
- **Loop guard.** Two bots can pass at most **12 messages in 10 minutes** (either direction), a chain can be at most **8 bots deep**, and a hand-off that sends the same text back to a bot already on the chain is refused. A held hand-off is not delivered; the caller gets a one-sentence "Held: …" and a **loop guard** item appears in your attention list. Terminal (gizzi) bots get the same 12-in-10-minutes guard from their inbox.
- **Retries are safe.** The tool sends an idempotency key made from the session and the tool call, so a retried call returns the earlier result instead of asking the bot twice.
- **One trace.** The hand-off carries a W3C `traceparent` header, so the whole chain (for example Grok to A:// to Gizzi) shares one trace id.


**On a call.** When you talk to a bot by voice in full screen, the work it has handed to other bots in the last day shows under its steps, one chip per bot: **Dev · working**, **Inbox · done**. Talk to one front-desk bot (for example A://) and watch who picked up what.

## Timeline

The thread pane has a **Timeline** section: the hand-offs, events and model windows of the thread as one tree. Each hand-off shows its receipt, how long it took, and the model and tokens where they were recorded. It is built from Allternit's own ledger, so it works with OpenTelemetry off. Cost per hop and OpenTelemetry spans are not part of it yet.

## Context generations

A durable thread can span several model sessions, called generations. A handoff checkpoints the current work, opens a fresh session, and seeds it with the checkpoint. The transcript marks the boundary with a divider and summary. Earlier generations remain part of the work history.

Usage reporting can request a handoff when the current context reaches 70 percent of its model window, including after switching to a smaller window. The thread id remains stable; `currentSessionId` can change. Integrations should keep the thread id as their work reference.

## Subagents

Subagents delegate a portion of a thread's work and appear in its subagent tree/feed. Inspect their activity and results alongside the parent thread. A subagent is distinct from a reusable bot and does not automatically become a project team member.

## Runtime API

These authenticated routes belong to the private Allternit API data plane, normally on port 8013. Do not assume they are public Cloud API endpoints; see [API overview](/api/overview).

| Method | Runtime path | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GET / POST | `/api/v1/threads` | List or create threads. |
| GET / PATCH | `/api/v1/threads/:id` | Read or update a thread. |
| GET | `/api/v1/threads/by-session/:session_id` | Find the durable thread for a session. |
| POST | `/api/v1/threads/:id/resolve` | Resolve a task thread. |
| POST | `/api/v1/threads/:id/usage` | Report model context usage. |
| POST | `/api/v1/threads/:id/handoff` | Checkpoint and create a fresh generation. |
| PUT | `/api/v1/threads/:id/deps` | Set dependencies. |
| GET | `/api/v1/threads/:id/events` | Read lifecycle events. |
| POST | `/api/v1/bot-routing/roster` | `{sessionId}` → the bot behind a bot chat session and the account's other bots. 404 when the session is not a bot's chat. |
| POST | `/api/v1/bot-routing/message` | `{sessionId, target, message, waitSeconds?, idempotencyKey?}` plus an optional `traceparent` header → start or continue the target bot's task thread; returns `status` (`done` with `reply`, `started`, or `failed`), `threadId`, `bot` and `receipt` (`{state, hopId, reason?}`). A replayed `idempotencyKey` returns the earlier result with `replayed: true`. 429 when the loop guard holds the hand-off. |
| GET | `/api/v1/threads/:id/timeline` | The thread's span tree: `{threadId, traceIds, spans: [{kind: hop \| event \| generation, state, latencyMs, model, tokensIn, tokensOut, children}]}`. |

Continue with [project coordination](/guides/project-coordination) or [artifact modes](/guides/artifact-modes).

## Thread top and reading the history

The thread top uses one row: the bot pill, the vendor provenance chip when connected, and thread controls. Tap the avatar to open the bot screen. Tap the bot name or progress to open the Thread pane. Tap the thread title to [switch threads](#switching-threads). Task threads also offer **Resolve**.

The **via Vendor ✓** chip identifies the connected vendor. The check indicates verified connection identity; it does not verify the answer. Open the chip to see the agent, connection mode, lane, guarantee level, connection health, and channel when present. A browser lane can be best effort even when the account is verified.

A ripped-page divider marks a new context generation. It shows what was carried forward and the checkpoint when available. A plan card shows the task objective and checklist. The wavy rule marks work picked back up after a time gap; project conversations also use a wavy day separator. These markers describe history, not task completion.

## Thread, Browser, and Computer pane

Open the thread pane from the pill or pane control, or open the computer from its control. The pane's title menu switches between **Thread**, **Browser**, and **Computer**. Thread has two sections: **Activity** (with a **List** / **Timeline** switch) and **About this thread** (objective, where things stand, decisions, open items, kind, dependencies and context windows). The thread's plan and subagents are in the [Tasks tab](#the-deck-above-the-composer); its artifacts are in the chat and in **Bot → Artifacts**, and an artifact you open from the chat shows in the pane. Browser and Computer show this thread's browser and computer surfaces. On Desktop, the pane can dock, resize, and expand. While it is open, notifications and Edit bot fold into **⋯** beside Resolve; the pane supplies its own view menu and close control.

On the phone, the avatar and provenance chip stay in the top row. Use the pill for Thread, or **⋯ → Computer** for the computer. The pane opens full screen. Its slim view bar switches Thread / Browser / Computer; **Back** returns to chat. The same controls are used by the `ai.allternit.com` phone layout and the `m.allternit.com` PWA. The computer status row wraps to fit the screen.
