---
title: What your bot can do on its own
description: "Choose, for each place your bot talks, whether it drafts, asks first, sends and tells you, or acts within limits you set. Payments always need your OK."
---

Your bot can text, call, email and post in chat channels for you. **Autonomy** is how far it goes without checking with you, set separately for each place, and for particular people if you want.

The same controls are on Allternit Desktop, on ai.allternit.com and on the m.allternit.com phone app.

## The four levels

| Level | What the bot does | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Draft only** | Writes the message and stops. Nothing is sent. | The draft in your Inbox, so you can send it yourself. |
| **Ask first** | Holds every message until you approve it. | A card in your Inbox with the message and an approval. Approve and it goes out once. |
| **Send and tell me** | Sends right away. | A line in your Inbox saying what was sent and to whom. |
| **Act within limits** | Sends on its own while inside your limits, then asks. | Nothing while it is inside the limits. Past a limit it behaves like Ask first. |

**Payments always need your approval**, at every level. Draft only keeps a payment as a draft. The other three ask you first.

## Defaults

If you have not set anything, a bot uses these:

| Place | Default |
| --- | --- |
| Email | Ask first |
| Texts | Send and tell me |
| Calls | Send and tell me |
| Chat channels (Telegram, Slack, Discord, Teams, WhatsApp) | Act within limits, up to 200 messages a day |

## Set it for a place

1. Open the bot's phone (see [Your bot's phone](/guides/bot-phone)).
2. In an app such as **Mail**, **Messages**, **Phone** or a channel like **Telegram**, tap the chip under the header. It shows the current level, for example **Ask first**.
3. Pick a level. It saves straight away.

To set every place in one list, open the phone's **Settings** app and use **What your bot can do on its own**. **Use the default** puts a place back to the table above.

## Limits

Choosing **Act within limits** shows three settings:

- **Messages per day.** Counted per bot and place, per UTC day. Messages you approve count too. Leave it empty for no cap.
- **Spend per day.** In dollars. Leave it empty for no cap.
- **Allowed actions.** Messages, calls and booking. Anything not ticked is held for your approval.

When a limit is reached the bot does not stop working. The next item waits in your Inbox as **needs your OK**.

## Exceptions for a person

In **Settings → What your bot can do on its own → Exceptions for a person**, enter a phone number, email address or handle, choose where, and press **Add (Ask first)**. Then change that person's level in the same place. A person's setting beats the bot's setting for that place, which beats the all-bots setting.

## Where you see it

Everything the bot held, drafted or told you about lands in your Inbox. Open the card to go to the conversation. Holds that need your OK also appear as the usual approval card.

## What this bot may do at all

On the bot's page, **What this bot may do** sets an outer limit that your levels cannot go past:

| Envelope | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Read only** | The bot looks things up and answers you. It sends nothing. |
| **Draft only** | It writes the message; you send it. |
| **Ask first** | It asks you before every send or action. |
| **Act within limits** | It acts on its own wherever your levels allow. This is the default. |

Built-in bots come with one that fits the job. Bots that contact clients start on **Ask first**. Payments and contracts always need your OK, whatever the envelope. See [Prompt injection defense](/guides/injection-defense) for what else holds a send.

## Allow for this task

Sometimes you want your bot to stop asking for one job. You can give it a **one-task grant**: for the rest of that thread, the kinds of actions you allow go out without a new approval card. You get a line in your Inbox for each one instead, like Send and tell me.

There are three ways to give one:

- **On an approval card**, press **Allow for this task** beside Approve. This approves the request in front of you and allows that kind of request for the rest of the task.
- **On a plan card**, pick **Approve plan and allow its sends and uploads**. The bot starts the plan, and its messages and file uploads go out without asking again.
- **In your own words.** Tell the bot in the thread, for example "you have my permission to send the reply and upload the file". The bot records exactly the actions you named: send, upload, submit, booking or call.

While a grant is live, the thread header shows **Allowed for this task: send, upload · Revoke**. Press **Revoke** to end it right away. A grant also ends when the thread is resolved, archived or paused, and after 7 days at the latest.

A grant never covers everything:

- **Payments, signatures and deletions always ask**, grant or not.
- **Outside content still asks.** If the conversation includes an email, web page or message from someone outside your team, every send needs your OK, and a grant can't be given in words in that thread. Use the card button instead.
- **Secrets are still blocked** and personal data still asks, as described below.
- A grant from your words only counts when the latest message in the thread is your own. Text the bot read in an email, a page or a tool result can't grant anything.

## Good to know

- A call that needs your OK is not placed until you approve it. The bot is told to try again once you have.
- Email keeps the mailbox's own reply mode until you choose a level for it. Once you choose one, your level decides: **Send and tell me** and **Act within limits** reply directly, but only for a verified domain and never past the reply caps. **Ask first** and **Draft only** send the email to the review queue. Whatever the mode or level, a reply in a conversation with outside content (anyone below `team`, such as a contact or a stranger) goes to the review queue. See [Injection defense](/guides/injection-defense).
- AI apps and vendor bots that text, call or email through your bot follow the same levels.

See [Autonomy API](/api/autonomy) for developers, and [Prompt injection defense](/guides/injection-defense) for how content from outside holds a send.
