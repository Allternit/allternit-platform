---
title: Pages that act like apps
description: A page can save data, ask AI and use your connected apps. What it can do, what you approve, and who pays.
---

A **page** artifact can be more than a static page. It can remember things,
ask AI questions and call the apps you've connected. The page asks for each of
these up front, and you approve them the first time it uses one.

Pages that don't need any of this keep working as plain pages.

## What a page can do

| Capability | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Storage** | The page saves small bits of text (a to-do list, scores, settings). *Personal* data is only yours. *Shared* data is seen, and can be changed, by everyone who can open the page. |
| **AI** | The page asks AI a question and shows the answer, for example "summarize my notes". |
| **Connected apps** | The page reads from, or acts in, apps you've connected, such as a calendar or an issue tracker. |

A page can only use what it declared before you opened it. If it tries
anything else, the call fails and nothing happens.

## What you approve

The first time a page needs one of these, a sheet slides up over the page:

- **Shared data.** Asked before the page's first *shared* write. Reading shared
  data and using personal storage need no approval.
- **AI.** Asked before the page's first AI call.
- **Connected apps.** Asked before the page's first call to an app. The sheet
  lists every app and tool the page declared, each with its own switch. Turn a
  tool off and it stays off for you the next time the page runs.

Choose **Don't allow** and the page gets an error for that call; it can ask
again later. Your choices are kept per page and per person: approving a page
for yourself doesn't approve it for anyone else.

## Who pays

AI runs on **your** account, not the owner's. It uses your credits or your free
allowance and follows your usage limits, the same as asking in a chat. If you
are out of credits, the page shows that the call couldn't run.

Apps run with **your** sign-ins. The page never sees your credentials, only the
result of the call.

## What's limited

- **Storage is 20 MB of text per page**, counting everyone's data together. A
  single stored value can be at most 5 MB. When the page is full, saves fail
  until something is deleted.
- **Tools that ask for approval before each action aren't available in
  pages.** If an app's permission mode asks you every time (or the tool changes
  things), the page can't call it. Use the app in a chat instead.
- **Pages with AI or connected apps can't be shared with "Anyone with the
  link".** Link viewers aren't signed in, so there's nobody to bill and no
  account to use. Share with people or your org instead.
- **People outside your organization never get AI or connected apps** on your
  page. They can still open it and use storage.
- **Signed-out viewers** (opening a link) see "Sign in to use this" when the
  page tries storage, AI or an app.
- Your organization's admin can turn pages' use of connected apps off for
  everyone, or turn artifacts off altogether.

## Where it works

Pages run the same way on Allternit Desktop, ai.allternit.com, the phone layout
and the m.allternit.com app. The approval sheet is full width on a phone.
Organization and sharing settings are changed on Desktop or the web.

## Making a page that uses these

Ask for what you want ("a habit tracker that remembers my streaks", "a page that
summarizes my notes with AI"). Allternit writes the page and declares what it
needs. When you open your own page, its declaration is saved with it, so the
sharing window already knows it can't be a public link.

If you write the page yourself, see
[Page runtime API](/api/artifact-runtime) for the `window.allternit` calls and
how to declare capabilities.
