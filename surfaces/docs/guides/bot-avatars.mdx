Every bot has an avatar. It shows in bot chips, the rail, thread headers, and as the on-screen guide when the bot is selected.

## Choose a look

Open **Create bot** (or **Edit bot**) and go to **Look**. It has four tabs: **Library**, **Describe**, **Your photo** and **Initials**.

**Library** holds every character, one family at a time. **Search characters** looks across all of them.

| Family | What's in it | Can you change the colour? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yours | Characters you described, kept in this browser | Yes |
| Objects | A controller, barbell, mug, lightbulb, chat bubble, sprout and kettle | Yes |
| Shapes | Originals, Minimals and the 12 builder shapes | Yes |
| Gizzi | Gizzi, Gizzi Family, Gizzi YouTube and Gizzi Brawl | Gizzi only |
| Vendors | Clawd Crew, Duet Carry, Grok Carry and Codex Carry | No |
| Robots | Alloy Classic, Alloy Pro, Mecha, Space Corps, Retro Pixel and Cyber | No |
| Creatures | Creatures, Dot Friends, Magic, Nature and Orbs | No |
| People | Soft Vinyl and Office Professionals | No |

Drag a character to turn it. Press a state (Idle, Thinking, Working, Review, Happy, Wave, Walk, Failed or Sleep) to see how it moves.

Characters in Yours, Objects and Shapes take any **Colour**, **Finish** (Flat, Matte clay, Soft plush, Glossy vinyl, Glazed ceramic, Jelly or Brushed metal) and **Accessory**. Press **Use as avatar** to apply one; the bot keeps its current look until you do. Characters in the other families are finished art, so they apply as soon as you pick them.

### Describe a character

In **Describe**, type a hobby, a role or an object, such as "a small kettle" or "chess", and press **Make character**. The Allternit model draws the shape and the new character appears in **Yours**, ready for its colour, finish and accessory. You need to be signed in, and it counts as a normal model request on your account.

To use no Allternit model, choose **Use another chat instead**, copy the prompt into any chat app, then paste the reply back and press **Add character**. A reply that can't be used tells you what is wrong.

**Yours** stays in this browser and holds up to 24 characters. Removing one never changes a bot that already uses it.

Look works the same on Desktop, the ai.allternit.com phone layout and the m.allternit.com app. If a browser can't draw 3D graphics, the stage says so and the fixed-art families still work.

## The guide follows your bot

When you pick a bot (in the Bots top deck, the launchpad, or the chat composer), the bot takes the stage above the greeting in place of Gizzi. With no bot selected, Gizzi stays as before.

## Your bot's stage

The stage is a dark tile with a soft glow in the bot's color. Your bot stands on it with its own avatar, alive:

- It breathes, glances around now and then, and leans toward your pointer.
- Click it and it squishes, then voice starts with that bot. While voice is on, a click only makes it squish.
- While voice is on, it listens and speaks with you. It swells with your voice while it listens, and with its own voice while it speaks. Cast characters such as Goldie or Modem move their mouths as they talk. Gizzi and avatars built from eyes and a body look where you point.
- The line under the tile says what the bot is doing: Listening, Speaking, Muted, Thinking…, Working…, Needs you, or Something went wrong. It is empty when the bot is idle.

The tile shows on the main chat when a bot is picked. A bot's own chat and threads open on its [live stage](/guides/bot-threads#your-bots-stage) instead: the same living avatar, full screen on a wash of its color, with what it is saying, what needs you, its steps and what it made. On phones (the ai.allternit.com phone layout and the Allternit phone app) the tile is a little smaller. If your system is set to reduce motion, the bot stays still on its stage, and its state art and the status line still change. Characters from the built-in packs use high-resolution frames when they are shown big, so they stay sharp on the stage, in voice and as the desktop pet.

The same living avatar is the speaker on the [full-screen voice](/guides/voice#full-screen-voice) stage. When you talk with a bot, the voice bar under the thread shows that bot's avatar instead of the Allternit mark. When a bot is on a phone call, the call screen and the live call card show its avatar, speaking, listening or quiet after you take over.

### How animated avatars move

Animated avatars react to what their bot is doing, everywhere the bot appears (the rail, headers, the phone drawer and Bots screen): they run while a tool works, look thoughtful while the bot thinks or writes, wait when the bot needs you, wave when a reply finishes, jump when a plan finishes and slump when something fails. After five minutes with nothing going on they doze off. Mentioning a bot again makes its avatar wave. The Allternit orb works the same way: it moves while the main bot works or thinks, listens with a sweep that breathes with your voice, and melts and pulses while it speaks. A bot that needs you nudges side to side until you answer. In a bot's own chat, the bot's avatar also stands in for the Allternit orb on the working line: it thinks, searches, works and writes there while a turn runs, waits beside a question it asked, and rests under the finished reply. The Allternit orb stays the mark for the rest of the platform and for A:// itself. In a group chat, the resting mark under the last reply is that bot's avatar. Characters drawn with real frames for each state really walk and wave. Characters whose sheets don't have real frames hop instead of pretending to walk, so every avatar moves in a way that looks intended.

Characters with real frames for every state: the whole **Gizzi YouTube** cast (Gizzi, Dropsy, Crescent, rimraf, Yessir, Modem, Spicy, Gem, Ini, Goldie, the Acolyte and the Host), every **Gizzi Brawl** fighter and Super form (including Super Codex, Super Grok and Gizzi Lightning, new to the picker), and the four fuzzy dots in **Dot Friends**: Bleu, Lily, Sunny and Rosie. Bots already using one of these switch to the new frames by themselves.

## Desktop pet

Allternit Desktop can keep your bot on your desktop as a small pet that floats above other windows.

- **Click** it for quick work with that bot. **Double-click** it to show or hide the Allternit window. **Drag** it anywhere. **Right-click** for its menu: Quick chat, Pet, size and visibility, Reset position, Walk around when idle, and Hide desktop pet. To turn the pet on or off, open **Settings → Desktop pet** (under Desktop app) and use **Show pet on desktop**; the same page has Walk around when idle, Pet settings and Reset position. You can also bring a hidden pet back from the Allternit icon in the menu bar: **Show Desktop Pet**.
- It shows what your bots are doing, whichever bot is busy: it runs while a tool runs, looks things over while a bot thinks, waits when a bot needs you, waves when a reply finishes, jumps when a plan is done, and slumps when something fails. After five quiet minutes it falls asleep. Every avatar does this. Characters with drawn frames play them, and the rest (portraits, uploads, photos, generated avatars) hop, rock, slump, dim and spin.
- If a reply finishes while the Allternit window is hidden or in the background, the pet wears a mail badge. It goes away when you go back to Allternit or open quick chat.
- In **Pet, size and visibility** you pick which bot it is and how big it is (48 to 160 px). The Gizzi terminal pet wears the same bot.
- With **Walk around when idle** on (the default, also in **Pet, size and visibility**), the pet strolls along its row when nothing is happening and stays put while a bot works. Characters with drawn frames walk; the rest hop along. It never walks if your system is set to reduce motion. Wherever it stops is remembered.
- If a reply finishes in Allternit while you're elsewhere, a speech bubble says **Reply ready**. When a bot needs your answer, it says **Needs your answer**.

### Carry a bot onto anything

You can pick a bot up from the Bots rail and drop it on whatever you're looking at, then ask it about that.

- **Out of the app (Allternit Desktop):** drag a bot from the Bots rail (the main bot, a promoted bot or any bot in the list) past the edge of the Allternit window. The bot leaves the app and follows your pointer. Let go over another app's window, such as a chart, a spreadsheet or an email, and quick work opens right there with that bot.
- **What it sees:** quick work gets the app and window title it landed on as an attachment you can remove before you send. If Allternit already has the macOS **Screen Recording** permission, a picture of that one window is attached as well. Allternit never asks for the permission for this, and nothing is sent until you send your message.
- **Move it:** drag the bot onto another window and the attachment changes to that window. The conversation stays.
- **Send it home:** drag the bot back onto the Allternit window. Quick work closes and your desktop pet goes back to how it was (or hides again if it was off).
- **Inside the app (every surface):** drop a bot anywhere on the open view instead of out of the window, and a small quick chat opens beside it with the text of that view (the open report, thread or page) attached. On a phone, **press and hold** a bot in the drawer: the drawer fades away, the bot follows your finger, and letting go opens the quick chat as a sheet at the bottom (the drawer closes then). A quick tap still opens the bot as before.
- Press **Escape** during the drag to cancel. Press **Escape** or click outside to close the quick chat.

### Terminal agents

The pet also follows agents running in your terminal.

- **Gizzi** reports on its own while Allternit Desktop is running: thinking, running a tool, waiting for your permission, and a wave when a turn ends. To turn this off, set `GIZZI_DESKTOP_PET_HOOK=0`.
- **Claude Code, Codex and other tools** can use the hook script that Desktop writes next to its settings. On macOS that folder is `~/Library/Application Support/@allternit/desktop/`; on Linux it's `~/.config/@allternit/desktop/`. The script does nothing if Desktop isn't running, and it never holds up the agent for more than a second.

For Claude Code, add hooks to `~/.claude/settings.json`:

```json
{
  "hooks": {
    "UserPromptSubmit": [{ "hooks": [{ "type": "command", "command": "\"$HOME/Library/Application Support/@allternit/desktop/desktop-pet-hook.sh\" thinking claude" }] }],
    "PreToolUse": [{ "matcher": "*", "hooks": [{ "type": "command", "command": "\"$HOME/Library/Application Support/@allternit/desktop/desktop-pet-hook.sh\" tool claude" }] }],
    "PostToolUse": [{ "matcher": "*", "hooks": [{ "type": "command", "command": "\"$HOME/Library/Application Support/@allternit/desktop/desktop-pet-hook.sh\" tool-done claude" }] }],
    "Notification": [{ "hooks": [{ "type": "command", "command": "\"$HOME/Library/Application Support/@allternit/desktop/desktop-pet-hook.sh\" waiting claude" }] }],
    "Stop": [{ "hooks": [{ "type": "command", "command": "\"$HOME/Library/Application Support/@allternit/desktop/desktop-pet-hook.sh\" turn-done claude" }] }]
  }
}
```

For Codex, add this to `~/.codex/config.toml`. Use your full home path, because Codex doesn't expand `~`:

```toml
notify = ["/Users/you/Library/Application Support/@allternit/desktop/desktop-pet-hook.sh", "turn-done", "codex"]
```

Codex only reports finished turns, so with Codex the pet waves but doesn't run.

To make the pet say something from a script, run `desktop-pet-hook.sh bubble "Tests pass"`.

<Note>
Terminal states fade on their own: running and thinking last two minutes unless the agent reports again, so a closed terminal never leaves the pet stuck. A terminal agent's reply doesn't add the mail badge, because there's nothing waiting in Allternit.
</Note>

## Create bot at a glance

**Create bot** opens on the look. At the top you see your bot's avatar, live: it changes as soon as you pick a colour, a character or a photo. The colours sit right under it, and under those the four ways to make a look: **Build**, **Characters**, **Photo** and **Initials**. Click one to open it in place, or click **Edit look**. Click **Done** to fold it away.

Next to the avatar (under it on a narrow window or a phone) are the fields: **Name — Role** (the only one you must fill in) and **What it does** (pick a role, or describe the job and click **Draft instructions from this**). **More options** still holds Instructions, Tools, Runs on and Model.

At the very top, switch between **Build a new bot** and **Bring a vendor's bot**.

On a phone the Create sheet is unchanged: avatar first, name, characters, colours, then More options.

## Avatar packs

In **Create bot** (under **Characters**) or **Edit bot → Look**, choose a pack and a character. Some characters have animated sheets (idle, walk, run, jump, sad, sleep, action, and a signature move); the rest are still portraits.

Vendor bots use their vendor's own mark or the avatar the vendor supplies. See [Vendor bots](/guides/vendor-bots).

Some vendors also have a family of their own under **Look → Characters → Vendors**, offered first when you make a bot for that vendor: **Grok Carry** (xAI, led by Gray Pip, the gray Pip that Grok wears in the bot picker; Originals has it too), **Codex Carry** (OpenAI), **Clawd Crew** (Claude and Claude Code) and **Duet Carry** (Claude Duet).

- **Clawd Crew** is eight flat pixel Clawds, the Claude Code crab, in different colours and hats: Clawd, Pinch, Scuttle, Tidepool, Molt, Barnacle, Shelly and Inky. Their claws go up while they work, and their eyes turn to ^ ^ when they finish.
- **Duet Carry** is eight clay-coloured shapes, each with one ink line that loops into eyes: Coda, Lyra, Trill, Hum, Riff, Echo, Tempo and Cadence. Their faces follow Duet's own mascot: a wave while working, a loop while thinking, and two dots when done.

Each character has blink, working, speaking and done faces and an animated sheet. Both families are Allternit's own drawings, not Anthropic's logos.

## Build a character

Make an original 3D character for your bot in a few seconds, with no account,
key, upload or AI. It's drawn by your browser on your device and is free.

1. Open **Create bot** (or **Edit bot**) and go to **Look**.
2. Choose **Build a character** (**Build** in Create bot). Drag the preview (or focus it and use the arrow
   keys) to turn it.
3. Work through **Shape → Color → Finish → Accessory**:
   - **Shape:** Round, Bean, Cloud, Star, Heart, Droplet, Cube bot, Cat, Bear,
     Bunny, Owl, Ghost
   - **Color:** nine swatches, or **Custom color**
   - **Finish:** Matte clay, Soft plush, Glossy vinyl, Glazed ceramic, Jelly,
     Brushed metal
   - **Accessory:** None, Cap, Beanie, Headphones, Glasses, Bow, Crown, Leaf
     sprout, Antenna, Scarf, Star badge, Flower
4. Press **Use as avatar**, then **Create** or **Save**. If you change the
   character afterwards, press **Use as avatar** again. Create and Save wait
   until you do.

Your character becomes the bot's picture everywhere (chips, the bot rail, thread
headers). Above the composer and as the desktop pet it animates like the sprite
packs: it idles and blinks, waves, jumps, slumps when something fails, waits for
you, works, looks things over, sleeps and spins. When the desktop pet strolls it
turns and walks the way it's going. Characters made before October 2026 keep
their older animation until you open them in **Edit bot** and press **Use as
avatar** again. **Edit bot** reopens the builder on the
same character. With *reduce motion* on, the preview stays still.

<Note>
The builder needs WebGL. If your browser can't draw 3D graphics, the tab says so
and offers the sprite packs, which work everywhere.
</Note>

## Make an avatar from a photo

Open the bot's **Config → Edit bot → Look → Your photo** (or choose **Photo** while creating a bot).

1. Upload a PNG, JPEG, or WebP (up to 15 MB). On a phone you can pick from your camera roll.
2. Choose a style: **Pixel sprite**, **Chibi**, or **Mascot**.
3. Confirm that the photo is of you, or that you have the person's permission.
4. Choose a lane:
   - **Included with your plan:** shown first when you're signed in. Plus includes 5 photo avatars a month, Super 25, Ultra 50. Allternit pays for these; they don't use your money or your credits. The lane shows how many are left this month ("3 of 5 left this month"). An avatar only counts if one is made, so a failed try doesn't use one. When they're used up, the lane shows the date they come back (the 1st of the next month, UTC) and you can still use the other two lanes. Without a Plus, Super or Ultra plan the lane is shown turned off with **See plans**. If it says **Included avatars are paused this month**, Allternit has paused the included lane for everyone; use another lane.
   - **ChatGPT subscription:** needs a running Sessions computer, accepted ChatGPT terms in **Settings → Connections**, and a ready ChatGPT account. If ChatGPT shows a security check, open ChatGPT on your Sessions computer, finish the check, then press **Check ChatGPT again**.
   - **OpenAI key:** uses your connected key and shows the estimated cost per try. Each approved try can be billed.
5. Press **Make sprite**. This prepares an approval card and sends nothing yet. Only **Approve and make avatar** sends the photo. **Cancel** sends nothing.
6. Keep the editor open while it runs. Small squares pulse in the preview while your sprite is made, then the portrait fades in. Review it, then save the bot.

The photo itself is not saved on the bot. The bot keeps the generated image, the style, and the time you confirmed permission. **Make another** needs a fresh approval. If a try fails, the editor shows what to do. It never switches to a different (paid) lane on its own.

Photo avatars are still portraits; animated sheets aren't made from photos.

## For developers

- **Resolution:** `resolveBotAvatarConfig` (`src/lib/agents/bot-avatar-config.ts`) reads the edited `config.avatar` first, then the decoded legacy column, then the deterministic profile. `BotAvatar` and `ActiveAgentMascot` share it.
- **Mascot:** `useActiveSurfaceAgent(surface)` (`src/lib/agents/surface-agent-context.ts`) resolves which bot represents a surface (surface selection, then open bot-thread metadata, then none). `<ActiveAgentMascot>` (`src/views/bots/ActiveAgentMascot.tsx`) renders `VendorAgentAvatar` for vendor bots, animates sheet avatars (`avatar.pet.spriteUrl`) on canvas driven by `MascotActivityState`, and otherwise delegates to `BotAvatar`. Callers: `AgentModeGizzi` and `BotStage` (the launch header and empty bot chats). It honors a look picked for the A:// main bot, the same rule as `BotAvatar` (`showsPrimaryOrb`).
- **Sheet engine:** sheets follow the [Petdex](https://github.com/crafter-station/petdex) standard: 8 columns of 192×208 cells. `src/lib/agents/pet-sheet.ts` defines the eleven pet states (`idle`, `running-right`, `running-left`, `waving`, `jumping`, `failed`, `waiting`, `running`, `review`, plus our `sleep` and `special`) with Petdex row order, frame counts and frame times. `detectPetLayout` reads a sheet's layout from its height: 11 rows is `petdex-v2`, a 9-row sheet under `/gizzi-brawl/` is `legacy-brawl-9` (idle, working, done, walk, jump, thinking, sleep, waiting, error), and any other 9-row sheet is `legacy-pack-9` (idle, blink, walk, run, jump, sad, sleep, action, special). `petRowFor` maps a state to a row. Legacy sheets have no real locomotion frames, so moving states play the idle row with a code hop (`petHopAt`), mirrored for `running-left`. `AnimatedBotSprite` takes either a pet state or the older `MascotActivityState` (`petStateFromMascot` maps `working` → `running`, `done` → `waving`, `error` → `failed`) and an optional `layout` hint.
- **Pet activity:** `src/lib/agents/pet-activity.ts` is the one signal for what agents are doing. Sources call `reportPetActivity(key, signal, { botId })` with `thinking`, `tool`, `tool-done`, `waiting`, `turn-done`, `plan-done`, `failed` or `idle`; readers use `usePetState(botId?)`, `getPetState` or `subscribePetState`. Thinking shows `review`, a running tool `running`, blocked on the person `waiting`; a finished turn waves, a finished plan jumps and a failure slumps, each for a few seconds (`PET_ONE_SHOT_MS`). When several sources are active the pet shows the most important (`PET_PRIORITY`: failed, waiting, running, review, jumping, waving). After 5 minutes with no activity it sleeps. Every `createModeSessionStore` store reports turn start and end from its `streamingBySession` flags (Stop goes idle, not a wave) and tool calls from the stream; `useThreadStore` reports bot and vendor thread status changes, skipping incognito threads and threads already finished when loaded.
- **Living avatars (stage and voice):** `BotAvatar` and `ActiveAgentMascot` take `live`. `BotAvatarMotion` then wraps the art in `LiveAvatarLayer` (`src/views/bots/LiveAvatarLayer.tsx`). The layer writes one transform per frame from the shared avatar frame clock: a lean toward the pointer (or the state's own look: thinking looks up, listening looks at you, a running tool scans), a squish spring on click, and a swell with the voice level. It also sets `--voice-level` for the A:// orb. It shares the gaze through `useAvatarGaze()`, which Gizzi (`attention`) and `AgentAvatar` (`lookAt`) read; it re-renders them at most about 12 times a second. Reduced motion turns the layer off.
- **Voice presence:** `src/lib/voice/voice-presence.ts`. `VoiceSession` takes `botId` and calls `setVoicePresence({ botId, phase, muted, level, outputLevel })` on every phase change, then `clearVoicePresence(botId)` on unmount. The phase is one of `listening`, `thinking`, `speaking` or `connecting` (`presencePhase` maps the voice phases). `useVoicePresence(botId)` returns it only for that bot, and `readVoiceLevel()` reads the mic while listening and the bot's voice while speaking, inside animation loops, never from the store. `BotAvatarMotion` reads it for every avatar of the bot: speaking plays `speaking`, voice thinking plays `review`, muted plays `sleep`. A running tool or a waiting question still wins. `orbStateFor` (`BotAvatar.tsx`) is the one orb mapping. Voice thinking also reports `thinking` to the pet signal under the key `voice`, so the Desktop pet follows it. While the bot speaks, pack characters with mouth art (`expressions.speaking`) switch between mouth art and rest art on the level (`SpeakingFlap` in `PackAvatarImage.tsx`).
- **Stage:** `src/components/bots/stage/BotStage.tsx`, used by `LaunchHeader` (150 px tile, which fits the fixed header zone) and the empty conversation view of `BotChatSessionView` (164 px; 132 px on phones). Bot chats open on `BotLiveStage` (`src/components/bots/stage/live/`), which renders `ActiveAgentMascot` `live` at up to 440 px without the tile; see [Your bot's stage](/guides/bot-threads#your-bots-stage). A click dispatches `VOICE_START_EVENT` with `{ threadId }` (a saved bot thread), `{ botId }` (a bot chat with no thread yet) or nothing (the main chat). `ThreadComposer` starts voice for a matching `threadId` or `voice.botId`; `ChatComposer` ignores events that carry either. `stageStatus` gives the status line. Stories: `BotStage.stories.tsx`.
- **Working mark:** `WorkingMark` (`src/components/brand/WorkingMark.tsx`) is the orb slot in transcripts: `ProcessBlock` (live status row and running steps, so `WorkingChamber` and `RenderingCard` too), `StreamingBubble`, `ActivityGlyph` (thought trace, todo widget, glass pill), `AskingQuestionStatus` and `RunFooter`. It renders `AllternitOrb` (or `SmoothOrb` with `smooth`) unless `WorkingMarkContext` holds a renderer. `BotWorkingMarkProvider` (`src/views/bots/BotWorkingMark.tsx`) wraps `BotChatSessionView` (desktop, the phone layout and the PWA's `FabricBotMode`) and the group chat's resting mark. It provides a renderer only when `showsPrimaryOrb(bot)` is false, so A:// keeps the orb. `BotWorkingMark` draws `<BotAvatar state>` at 1.4× the slot without moving the row; `avatarStateForOrb` maps thinking→`review`, searching→`running`, working→`working`, writing/speaking→`speaking`, idle→`idle`. Smooth slots hold each state 900 ms (`useDwell`). Tests: `src/components/brand/__tests__/working-mark.test.tsx`.
- **Calls:** `CallBotFace` (`src/components/gateway/phone/CallBotFace.tsx`) renders the bot's avatar on `CallParties` and the live `CallCard` when the caller passes `botId` (`CallScreen`, `ChannelThreadPane`, `SmsThread`, `PhoneCallsApp`). Without a bot it falls back to the AllternitOrb.
- **In-app reactions:** `BotAvatarMotion` (every bot avatar) reads `usePetState(botId)` when no explicit `state` is passed, and provides it to the sprite inside. `ActiveAgentMascot` reads `usePetState(botId, { unattributed: true })`, so plain chat turns with no bot attached move the surface's own mascot. The A:// orb maps `running` to working and `review` to thinking. Components take either vocabulary (`AvatarState` in `pet-sheet.ts`); `mascotStateFrom` maps pet states back to the app states (`idle`, `working`, `speaking`, `waiting`, `done`, `error`) for pack expression art and CSS motions. `waiting` (needs you) has its own nudge motion. Re-mentioning a bot reports a wave for it from `ChatComposer`.
- **Desktop pet (Electron):** `surfaces/allternit-desktop/src/main/desktop-companion.ts` owns the pet window, and `pet-activity-hub.ts` decides what it plays. The app window sends its pet state with `allternitCompanion.activity.report(state)` (IPC `companion:activity`, accepted only from the main window). Sources are combined in priority order, with optional expiry for sources that can vanish (the hook port). The pet window gets `{ state, unread, moving }` on `companion:activity` and can fetch it with `activity.get()`. `unread` is set when a turn finishes (`waving`) while the main window isn't focused, and is cleared when the main window gains focus or quick chat opens. `allternitCompanion.toggleMain()` (`companion:toggle-main`) shows or hides the main window. Roaming (`roam` setting, default on) walks the window along its row at 70 px/s to a point at least 120 px away, after a 6 to 18 s idle pause. It stops for any activity, a drag, quick chat or the HUD, and saves the stop position. Roaming waits for the pet page to send its reduced-motion preference (`activity.setReducedMotion`, IPC `companion:motion-pref`), so pages without walk support never move.
- **Sharp at any size:** every built-in pack sheet (192×208 cells) and 512 px portrait has a 2× copy on runtime.allternit.com (`assets/avatar-packs/<path>@2x.webp`, R2 bucket `allternit-runtime`), listed in `src/views/bots/avatar-hires.json`. Sheets were upscaled with Real-ESRGAN's anime-video model, and portraits with `realesrgan-x4plus` then halved. `AnimatedBotSprite` loads a sheet's 2× copy through `loadSheetImage` when it draws more than 1.25× its frame height in device pixels. `AvatarPortraitCanvas` loads a portrait's through `loadPortraitImage` above about 600 px. Both read the cell size from the image and fall back to the 1× art offline. The 2× files are not in git. For new pack art, run `REALESRGAN_DIR=… scripts/upscale-avatars.sh`, which upscales, uploads and lists them. `avatar-hires.test.ts` fails while a pack sheet is missing from the list. The bucket's CORS allows ai.allternit.com, m.allternit.com and Desktop (`127.0.0.1:8013`).
- **Avatars without drawn frames:** `src/views/bots/PortraitPetMotion.tsx` gives them free motion for each pet state, the way Petdex hops pets that have no sheet. This covers portrait-only pack characters, uploads and photo sprites (through `PackAvatarImage`), geometric and pet-face avatars, the Gizzi mascot and `AgentAvatar` configs. The motions are: `hop` for running and walking (mirrored for `running-left`), `jump`, `wave`, `wait`, `review`, `failed` (desaturated slump), `sleep` (dimmed) and `spin` for `special`. It reads the state from `BotAvatarMotion`'s context or a `state` prop, and it only runs from `PORTRAIT_MOTION_MIN_SIZE` (40 px) up. While it runs, it switches off `BotAvatarMotion`'s own breathing (`:has(.ppm)`). With reduced motion it is paused. Characters with a sheet never get it.
- **Desktop pet (page):** `allternit-ai/src/components/desktop-companion/DesktopCompanionBridge.tsx` (main window) reports the overall `getPetState()` on mount and on every change through `subscribePetState`, for all bots, not only the pet's own. `src/pages/DesktopCompanionPage.tsx` (`?surface=pet`) reads `{ state, unread, moving }` with `useCompanionActivity()` (`bridge.ts`; malformed payloads are dropped by `parseCompanionActivity`) and plays `petPlayState(activity)`: `running-left` / `running-right` while idle and moving, otherwise the state. It passes that to `<BotAvatar state>`, which then ignores the pet window's own (empty) activity store; the A:// orb follows it too. A click waits `PET_DOUBLE_CLICK_MS` (250 ms): a second click calls `toggleMain()`, otherwise quick chat opens. Desktop builds without `toggleMain` open quick chat at once; builds without `activity` show the still pet. Tests: `src/components/desktop-companion/__tests__/desktop-pet.test.tsx`.
- **Desktop pet hook port:** `surfaces/allternit-desktop/src/main/pet-hook-server.ts`, started at launch on `127.0.0.1:7787` (`PORTS.PET_HOOK`). If something else holds that port, it uses any free port. On start it writes `<userData>/desktop-pet-hook.json` as `{ "version": 1, "url", "port", "token" }` with mode 0600. The token is 32 random bytes and is kept across restarts. Beside it goes `desktop-pet-hook.sh` (mode 0700). Every request needs `Authorization: Bearer <token>`, a `Host` of `127.0.0.1:<port>` or `localhost:<port>`, and no `Origin` header, so browsers and DNS rebinding are refused. Bodies are JSON, 4 KB at most.
  - `POST /state` takes `{ "state", "client"?, "ttlMs"? }`. `state` is a signal (`thinking`, `tool`, `tool-done`, `waiting`, `turn-done`, `plan-done`, `failed`, `idle`) or a pet state. It becomes source `hook:<client>` in the activity hub. Every state expires: ongoing states after 2 minutes (`waiting` after 10), one-shots as in the app, and `ttlMs` is clamped to 1 s–10 min. `idle` clears the source.
  - `POST /bubble` takes `{ "text", "client"?, "ttlMs"? }`. The text is cut to one line of at most 140 characters. It shows for 1.5–30 s, default 5.
  - `GET /health` returns `{ "ok": true }`.
  - Errors: 401 bad token, 403 Origin or Host, 400 bad JSON or unknown state, 413 too large.
- **Carry a bot (Desktop):** `BotModeRail.tsx` runs rail drags with pointer capture. When `allternitCompanion.carry` exists and the pointer leaves the window (`isOutsideWindow`, `src/lib/bots/bot-carry.ts`), the rail sends `carry('start', botId)` (IPC `companion:carry`, accepted only from the main window). It sends `back` if the pointer comes back inside, `drop` on release and `cancel` on Escape. In `desktop-companion.ts`, `carryStart` saves what the pet was before (`away`), switches the pet to that bot, makes it click-through and moves it to the cursor every 16 ms. A carry that never ends stops after 2 minutes. `carryEnd('drop')` over another window saves the position and calls `openHud(agentId, petBounds)`: `unified-main.ts` `placeHudUnderPet` puts the HUD under the pet so `attachPetToHud` leaves the pet where it landed. Then `onPetLanded(petBounds)` runs. A drop over the main window, `back`, `cancel`, or a later pet drag that ends over the main window calls `goHome`, which closes the HUD and restores `away`. Tests: `desktop-companion.test.ts` (carrying a bot out of the app).
- **Carry context (Desktop):** `onPetLanded` calls `readBotCarryContext` (`src/main/bot-carry-context.ts`). It uses `readWindowBelow` at the pet's centre for the app, the title and the OS window id. In allternit-ai, `src/shell/hud/carry-context.ts` turns it into one composer attachment (`carry-<seq>`, a `screenshot` with the picture or a `document` without one, with the app and title as `extractedText`). `useCarryContextAttachment` in `ChatComposer` (HUD only) swaps it for the newest one. Tests: `carry-context.test.ts`.
- **Carry a bot (in the app):** a rail drop that lands on no slot or zone and outside `[data-bot-carry-ignore]` (the rail, the quick chat) calls `dropBotInApp`. `readDropContext` takes the nearest `[data-bot-drop-region]`, dialog, article, main or section under the point, names it by `aria-label`, first heading or the page title, and uses its `innerText` (8,000 characters at most). `useBotCarry.open` shows `BotCarryLayer` (Escape and a press outside close it, listened for in the capture phase) (`src/components/bots/BotCarryLayer.tsx`, mounted in `ShellApp` and `PhoneProviders`). Wide screens get a 380 px card next to the drop point; under 640 px it is a bottom sheet. It wraps `CompanionChat` with `context`: a removable chip, sent once as an `attachmentsAsText` block. The transcript shows only the typed part. Phone: `startLongPressCarry` (420 ms hold, 10 px slop) listens on the touched row and on `window`, handling each event once, and draws the bot under the finger (`ghost`). The row must stay in the page until the finger lifts, because a touch whose element is removed stops reaching any listener. So while carrying, `html[data-bot-carrying]` makes `phone.css` fade the drawer layers (`.phone-root [data-contained]`) and let touches through, and turns body pointer events back on (Radix turns them off under an open drawer) so the drop can read what is under the finger. The browser picks scroll or not at touch start, and React's touch listeners are passive, so `BotModeRail` adds its own non-passive `touchmove` that blocks scrolling only while `data-bot-carrying` is set. After pick-up, a `pointercancel` is ignored and the touch events finish the drag. `onEnd` closes the drawer after the drop. Tests: `BotModeRail.test.tsx` (carrying a bot onto the view).

- **Photo pipeline:** **Make sprite** only prepares the card. Approval is the only call site of `generatePhotoSprite` (`src/lib/bots/photo-sprite.ts`). The ChatGPT lane mints a single-use person proof for `avatar.photo_sprite` and submits an `image.generate` task with an image input. The OpenAI lane uses `generateImagesMediaPlane` with `referenceImage`. - **Included lane (cloud API):** `GET /api/v1/avatars/photo/allowance` returns `{ available, reason, plan, limit, used, remaining, resetsAt }`, where `reason` is `not_configured`, `no_subscription`, `paused` or `allowance_exhausted`. `POST /api/v1/avatars/photo` takes `{ photo, style, consentAttestedAt }` and returns `{ image, remaining, limit, resetsAt }` (`image` is a base64 PNG). Errors: 400 bad input or no consent, 402 `no_subscription`, 429 `allowance_exhausted`, 503 `not_configured` or `paused`, 422 `provider_rejected`, 502 `provider_error`. Both take a Clerk session or an API token with the `compute` scope. The code is `cmd/allternit-cloud-api/src/routes/photo_avatars.rs`. Monthly limits are `photo_avatars_per_month` on the plan list in `billing_subscriptions.rs`. The server builds the prompt from the style, downscales the photo to 1024 px or less in memory, and calls OpenAI images edit (`gpt-image-1`, 1024×1024, medium). The source photo is never stored or logged. Each use is reserved in `photo_avatar_usage` (migration `044`) under a lane-wide advisory lock. It's marked `used` only when an image comes back and `released` otherwise. A reservation that's never settled stops counting after 10 minutes.
- **Included lane spend guard:** every reservation also checks the lane's total for the month across all users. Each image is counted at $0.042, against `ALLTERNIT_AVATAR_MONTHLY_BUDGET_USD` (default `4.00`). At the cap the lane reports `paused` instead of making a paid call. An OpenAI `insufficient_quota` or billing error releases the reservation and pauses the lane for 30 minutes. The key is `OPENAI_API_KEY` from the service's EnvironmentFile; `ALLTERNIT_AVATAR_OPENAI_API_KEY` overrides it. With no key, the allowance reports `not_configured` and the app hides the lane.

- **Build a character:**
  
  The builder saves a normal image avatar (`uri` is the 512×512 transparent portrait) with an animated sheet and its recipe:
  
  ```json
  {
    "type": "image",
    "uri": "data:image/webp;base64,…",
    "pet": { "spriteUrl": "data:image/webp;base64,…",
             "frameWidth": 192, "frameHeight": 208, "columns": 8, "rows": 11 },
    "character": { "source": "builder", "version": 1,
                   "shape": "bunny", "color": "#60a5fa",
                   "finish": "plush", "accessory": "scarf" }
  }
  ```
  
  The sheet is a Petdex v2 sheet, 1536×2288: 8 columns by 11 rows of 192×208
  cells. The rows, with the frames each uses, are idle (6, with a blink on frame
  3), running-right (8), running-left (8), waving (4), jumping (5), failed (8),
  waiting (6), running (6), review (6), sleep (8) and special (8). Unused cells
  are transparent. `CHARACTER_SHEET.rowSpecs` in `recipe.ts` maps each row to a
  pose in `scene.ts` (`stroll`, `wave`, `wait`, `review` and the original poses).
  The walk rows turn the character `CHARACTER_SIDE_YAW` (1.15 rad, about 65°)
  toward the direction of travel. A full 90° profile hid round characters' faces.
  The walk rows also use a side gait: arms swing forward and back, and feet step
  out in front. Sheets saved before this change are 1536×1872 (8×9, rows idle,
  blink, walk, run, jump, sad, sleep, action, special). Renderers tell the two
  apart by image height, so both keep playing. Avatar renderers need no special case: the portrait is
  `uri` and the animation is `pet`. `character` is only read by the editor to
  reopen the builder. Unknown ids or colors fall back to defaults. three.js loads
  lazily, only when the tab opens. Source: `allternit-ai/src/lib/bots/character-builder/`.
