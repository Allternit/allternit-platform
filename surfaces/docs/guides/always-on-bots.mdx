---
title: Keep a bot answering
description: Stay on when your computer sleeps, and answer on a backup brain when a vendor sign-in needs a check. Both are opt-in, per bot.
---

Two settings keep a bot answering when something goes wrong. Both are off until you turn them on, and both are shown to you every time they happen.

Open the bot, then **Settings → Stay on**. The card is the same on Desktop, on ai.allternit.com (phone layout) and on the m.allternit.com app.

## Always on: your computer is asleep

When your computer is asleep or offline, texts and channel messages for the bot wait for it. Turn on **Always on** and pick a **Fail over to** computer (one of your cloud computers) and those messages go there instead. The cloud computer is started if it is stopped.

- The switch happens only while your computer is offline. When it sends its next heartbeat (within a minute of waking), messages go back to it.
- Every switch is written to the bot's thread as a "Moved to your cloud computer while your computer was offline" line, so you can see which computer answered.
- The cloud computer needs your model sign-ins to answer. Vendor accounts you already host there keep working.
- No cloud computer yet? The picker says so, and messages wait for your computer as before (the cloud still tells you when texts are waiting).

## Fallback brain: a vendor session needs a check

A vendor bot (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and others) never switches brains on its own. If its session isn't ready, the bot stops and tells you to sign in again.

If you would rather it keep answering, enter a **fallback brain** as `provider/model`, for example `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5-5`. Then, when the vendor session is not ready, is blocked, or needs you to sign in:

- the bot answers on that brain instead of refusing;
- the reply starts with one line, "Answered by claude-sonnet-5-5 because your Claude session needs a check.";
- the thread records a "Switched lane" event with the reason.

The fallback brain uses your own provider usage, which is billed to you like any other model call. Leave the field empty to keep the stop-and-ask behavior.

## Limits

- Always on moves messages, not a half-finished turn: a turn already running on your computer when it goes offline is not resumed elsewhere.
- Spoken (voice call) turns on a vendor bot do not use the fallback yet; they stop and tell you.
- Fallback applies to the first message that finds the session not ready. The vendor session still needs your attention under Settings → Connections.

## For developers

- Settings live in the bot's `config`: `availability = {alwaysOn, failoverRuntimeId}` and `fallbackBrain`. No column.
- The runtime reports `failoverRuntimeId` in its 60 s heartbeat (`POST /api/v1/runtime-presence/heartbeat`). The cloud keeps it on `runtime_devices.failover_runtime_id` (migration `091`), picks the delivery computer for each queued channel message in `channel_inbound`, and records each switch in `runtime_failovers`. The next heartbeat's reply carries finished failovers; the runtime writes `bot_failovers` (V263) and a `bot.failover` event on the bot's standing threads.
- `GET /api/v1/runtime-presence` reports `wakeable: true` for cloud computers.
- Fallback lives in `gateway_runner::run_with_fallback`; it writes `lane.fallback {from, to, reason}`.
