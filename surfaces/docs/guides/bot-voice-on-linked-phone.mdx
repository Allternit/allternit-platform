---
title: Let your bot talk on its own phone
description: Wire a bot's real phone to the Mac running Allternit Desktop with a cable, and the bot answers calls on that phone, talks and listens, on the same call screen as a phone-number call.
---

If your bot has its own real phone (an Android phone connected [over wireless debugging](/guides/phone-for-your-bot), or an iPhone linked with [Phone Link](/guides/phone-link)), a cable between that phone and your Mac lets the bot answer calls on the phone and hold the conversation itself.

## What it does

- **The bot answers after one ring.** When a call comes in on the phone, Allternit Desktop waits one ring, answers it, and the bot starts talking.
- **The bot talks and listens through the cable.** The caller's voice goes from the phone's headset port into your Mac; the bot's voice goes from your Mac back into the phone's microphone.
- **Same call screen.** The call shows exactly like a call on an [Allternit number](/guides/calls-and-texts): the call screen with the orb, the live transcript, the call card in the thread, and the summary after the call. The header says which phone the call is on, for example "on Moto".
- **Listen and Take over** work the same way: listen in without the caller hearing you, or take over and talk yourself. See [Voice](/guides/voice#phone-calls-listen-and-take-over).
- **The phone's live view switches to the call.** If you have the phone's screen open in Allternit while a call is live on it, the screen view changes to the call screen with the orb. **Show phone screen** switches back to the phone's screen; **Show call** returns to the call. This works on Desktop, on ai.allternit.com and in the m.allternit.com phone app.
- **Calls the bot places** (with `phone.call.dial`, after your approval) use the cable too: once the call connects, the bot talks on it.

Every call opens with a line saying the caller is talking to an AI assistant. Recording is off unless you've set up consent in the bot's call settings.

## What you need

- **Allternit Desktop running on a Mac near the phone.** The audio passes through that Mac. If Desktop isn't running, the phone rings as normal and nobody answers.
- **The phone already connected to Allternit** (Android over wireless debugging, iPhone through Phone Link).
- **A cable kit for each phone:**
  1. **A USB-C to 3.5 mm headphone adapter with charging (PD) pass-through**, so the phone keeps charging while plugged in. For an iPhone with a Lightning port, use a Lightning to 3.5 mm adapter; it can't charge the phone at the same time.
  2. **A 3.5 mm TRRS headset splitter (CTIA wiring)** that splits the phone's single headset jack into a microphone jack and a headphone jack.
  3. **A USB-C external sound card for the Mac** with separate microphone and headphone jacks.
  4. **Two short 3.5 mm male-to-male cables.**
  5. **A 3.5 mm inline attenuator** for the cable that carries sound from the Mac into the phone's microphone. A phone mic expects a much quieter signal than a headphone output gives, and without the attenuator the caller hears distortion.

## Wire it up

1. Plug the headphone adapter into the phone, and the TRRS splitter into the adapter.
2. **Phone sound to the Mac:** connect the splitter's **headphone** jack to the sound card's **microphone** jack with one male-to-male cable.
3. **Mac sound to the phone:** connect the sound card's **headphone** jack to the attenuator, and the attenuator to the splitter's **microphone** jack with the second cable.
4. Plug the sound card into the Mac.

The phone should now treat the cable as a wired headset. You can check by playing something on the phone: the sound should stop coming from the phone's speaker.

## Turn it on

1. In Allternit Desktop, open **Settings → Subscriptions → Linked phones**.
2. Open the phone's **⋯** menu and choose **Call audio**.
3. Turn on **Bot answers calls**.
4. Pick the **bot** that should answer.
5. Pick the **Input (caller voice)** device: the sound card's microphone input.
6. Pick the **Output (bot voice)** device: the sound card's headphone output.

The settings are saved with that phone. Each phone has its own cable kit and its own settings.

On ai.allternit.com and the m.allternit.com phone app, **Call audio** shows whether it's on and which bot answers, with **Set up in Allternit Desktop**. Changing it needs Desktop, because the audio devices are on the Mac.

## Test it

Call the bot's phone from another phone. After one ring the bot should answer, say that it's an AI assistant, and then talk with you. Watch the call screen in Allternit: the transcript should show both sides. Try **Listen**, then **Take over**, then hand the call back.

## Limits in this version

- **Keypad tones, transfer and hold don't work on these calls.** The buttons tell you the action isn't supported on this line, and the call carries on. Use an Allternit number if the bot needs to get through phone menus or transfer calls.
- **Desktop must be running** on the Mac the phone is wired to.
- **Android** is answered and hung up over adb and is the more reliable path. **iPhone** is answered by tapping the on-screen Accept button through Phone Link, which is best effort: if the incoming-call screen doesn't show as expected, the call may not be answered. Settings shows how well each phone is supported.
- One call at a time per phone.

## What it costs

Calls on a linked phone count as **Cloud Voice minutes**, the same as talking to a bot with Allternit Cloud voice: Plus includes 100 minutes a month, then $0.08 a minute. See [Voice pricing](/guides/voice#pricing). Your phone plan's own call charges still apply.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| A **Needs you** notice: "Call audio cable not found for &lt;phone&gt;" | Desktop can't find the input or output device you picked, so it didn't answer. Check the sound card is plugged into the Mac, then reopen **Call audio** and pick the devices again. |
| The caller hears echo or distortion | Lower the phone's call volume, and check the attenuator is on the Mac-to-phone-mic cable, not the other one. |
| You hear the bot quietly or not at all on the phone side | Check the cable from the sound card's headphone jack goes through the attenuator into the splitter's microphone jack, and raise the Mac's output volume for the sound card a little. |
| The phone doesn't detect the headset (sound still comes from its speaker) | Make sure the splitter is TRRS with CTIA wiring, push each plug all the way in, and try the adapter on its own with ordinary headphones. Some cheap adapters only carry sound out, not a microphone. |
| The phone rings but the bot never answers | Check Desktop is running, **Bot answers calls** is on, a bot is picked, and the phone shows as connected in Settings. |
