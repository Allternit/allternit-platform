**ACI** means Allternit Computer Interface. It is the surface for observing and interacting with a computer used by an agent. A computer, a connector, and an MCP App supply different capabilities.

| Resource | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Bot computer | The computer associated with a bot's work: browser, desktop, and host tools. |
| Sessions computer | The contained machine that runs the subscription gateway and provider sign-in sessions. |
| MCP server / connector | Exposes callable tools and their authenticated service access. |
| MCP App | An interactive view returned by an MCP tool; see its integration status below. |

## A bot's computer

Open the computer from the bot thread into the ACI pane. The view separates the conversation from the live computer, with a computer header and browser navigation where available. Observe work, take control when you need to intervene, and hand control back when ready.

Computer control is separate from the thread's execution state. Use the thread's Stop action to interrupt a model turn; use the computer's controls to manage human interaction with the displayed machine. A running computer alone does not mean a bot is actively working.

Provisioning and available actions depend on the selected computer backend and your access. An attached computer does not automatically grant every tool or service permission.

## The agent cursor

When an agent moves or clicks on a computer you're watching, a cursor glides to the spot and shows a click ring. It's drawn over the screen in the viewer, so the real mouse pointer on that computer is never moved or restyled by it. It appears in every computer viewer: the Computer side of the browser pane and ACI, a bot's computer (pane, page and its own window), your cloud computer's screen, the sign-in screen in Subscriptions, and the full-screen computer view on your phone (ai.allternit.com and the m.allternit.com app), where it's drawn smaller.

- **Change the style or turn it off:** Settings → Computers → **Agent cursor**. Pick Signature arc (the default), Spring settle, Magnetic, Comet swoop, Adaptive, Classic glide, or Off. The choice is saved on this device.
- **Reduced motion:** with your system's reduce-motion setting on, the cursor doesn't glide. It appears at each point with the click ring.
- **Screen readers:** the drawing is hidden from assistive tech. A polite status line says what the agent did, for example "Agent clicked at 512, 300", at most every few seconds.
- The cursor hides after a few quiet seconds and comes back with the agent's next action. Typing and key presses have no point on the screen, so they don't move it.

### The bot rides the cursor

The bot that is driving rides along with the cursor, so you can always tell which bot is moving the pointer and what it's doing.

- **While it moves:** only the bot's avatar shows, just below and to the right of the cursor tip. It never covers the spot being clicked, and near the edge of the screen it moves to the other side.
- **When it stops or acts:** the badge opens to the bot's name and the step, for example "Raven · Clicking Send". The step is the bot's own few words about the action, or a plain description of it when the bot didn't give one.
- **Click the badge** for a card with the bot, its task, the current step, how long it has been running, and Pause and Take control. Click anywhere else to close it. On your phone the badge is smaller, shows the name only, and a tap opens the card as a sheet at the bottom of the screen.
- **On this Mac:** when a bot drives this computer itself, the badge appears next to the real mouse pointer, in a see-through layer over the whole screen. That layer can't take clicks (they'd go to the app the bot is using instead), so it shows the avatar and the name and step only. Open the run in the Allternit app for the card. The layer is left out of the bot's own screenshots and goes away a few seconds after the bot's last action.
- **When you take control,** the badge greys out and reads "You have control". It comes back when you hand control back.
- **Turn it off:** Settings → Computers → **Agent cursor** → **Show the bot on the cursor**. It's on by default. The cursor itself keeps the style you picked.

### For developers: the event it plays

The overlay plays `computer.action` events from the computer's events channel in allternit-api. The computer toolset executor (`POST /computers/:id/toolset`) emits one per pointer action:

```json
{ "type": "computer.action", "ts": 1760000000,
  "data": { "member": "left_click", "x": 812, "y": 404, "screen_w": 1920, "screen_h": 1080, "run_id": "run_…",
            "actor": { "agent_id": "bot_123", "name": "Raven" }, "step": "Clicking Send" } }
```

- `actor` and `step` label the bot badge. The caller sends `agent_id`, `agent_name` and `step` on the toolset request; without `agent_id` the computer's own bot is used, and without either `actor` is `null` and the viewer falls back to the run's bot, then your own bot, then a plain cursor. Details: [Computer toolset events](/tools/computer-toolset#events).

- `x` and `y` are real screen pixels (after the executor's model-frame scaling); `screen_w` and `screen_h` are that screen's size. The viewer maps them onto the displayed picture, accounting for letterboxing and the viewer's fit or actual-size mode.
- Click members (`left_click`, `right_click`, `middle_click`, `double_click`, `triple_click`, `left_mouse_down`) show the click ring. `mouse_move`, `left_click_drag`, `scroll` and other members with a point glide only. Members without a point are ignored.
- Live stream: mint a token with `POST /api/v1/computers/:id/ws-token` `{"purpose":"events"}`, then open `/ws/computers/:id/events?token=…`. It replays recent history on connect; the viewer skips events older than the moment it connected. If the stream can't open, the viewer polls `GET /api/v1/computers/:id/events?limit=10` while the page is visible and stops quietly after repeated errors.
- On this Mac, the ACI engine's own `cursor.moved` run-stream events (model frame, with `coordinate.contract`) drive the same overlay without a round trip. They carry `actor` (the bot the run was started for, or `null`) and `step_text` (the planned action's reason); their `step` field stays the step number.
- The badge next to the real pointer on this Mac is a Desktop window (`surfaces/allternit-desktop/src/main/agent-cursor-window.ts`) that loads `companion.html?surface=agent-cursor`. Its page uses the `window.allternitAgentCursor` bridge: `show()`, `hide()`, `getDisplay()` (width, height and scale factor of the primary display) and `onDisplay(callback)`.
- Motion comes from Cua's MIT `@trycua/cursor-motion` player, vendored in allternit-ai at `src/vendor/cua-cursor-motion/` with the upstream commit in its README.

## Which computer your phone and the web use

m.allternit.com, ai.allternit.com and other cloud surfaces send each request to one of your computers, called your default node. It's picked this way:

1. **Your pinned computer**, if it's online. When an Allternit cloud computer first pairs, it's pinned for you automatically, unless you already have a pin. This keeps your phone on the always-on computer instead of switching to your laptop whenever the laptop is awake.
2. Otherwise, a computer with a live connection to Allternit.
3. Otherwise, the computer seen most recently.

A computer counts as online if it checked in within the last 10 minutes and its credential hasn't expired. If your pinned computer is offline, requests go to the next one on the list rather than failing. The pin is stored per account in `user_node_preferences` (surface `*`). The resolver lives in `cmd/allternit-cloud-api/src/services/node_resolution.rs`.

## Coding tools on any computer

Settings → Connections › Coding tools installs and signs in CLI brains on the computer it names: this Mac, or the computer your phone and the web are using, such as your always-on Allternit cloud computer. Anything set up on a cloud computer keeps working while your own computer is closed. Step-by-step instructions are in [Subscriptions](/guides/subscriptions#coding-tools-on-any-computer).

## Restore points and backups for your cloud computer

Settings → Computers (and the Computers tab on your phone) has a **Restore points and backups** section for each Allternit cloud computer on your account.

- **Take restore point** saves the computer's disk as it is now. Take one before a big change, such as letting an agent install software or reorganize files. You can keep 5; delete an old one to take another.
- **Restore** rolls the computer back to that point. Anything changed since is lost. A running computer restarts, which takes under a minute.
- **Download a copy** packs the whole computer into one `.tar.zst` file. It takes a few minutes. When it's ready the button becomes **Download copy**, and the link works for an hour. The file can be imported on any Incus host with `incus import`.

Your cloud computer is also backed up off-site every night, encrypted, and kept for 8 days. Restore points don't replace that backup: they live on the same host as the computer.

## Connected tools

MCP tools belong to the session's tool belt. Connect the server, complete its authentication, and check the available tools before assigning work that relies on it. CLI harness sessions can receive the session's tools and instructions through the CLI tool bridge; supported computer operations still require a reachable host.

## Step history and replay

The shared viewer shows the current action, steps grouped by run, and a recorded screen after each action when capture and redaction succeed. Choose a past run, click a step, scrub **Replay step**, or play the run. **Return to live screen** closes the recorded screen. Replay only reads history; it cannot send mouse, keyboard or tool actions. Runs predating this feature do not gain historical screenshots.

Snapshots are redacted in required mode before saving. History stores action names, result and time, never raw tool inputs or typed text. It is account-authorized through the computer's existing access check, bounded to 500 steps per computer and 30 days, with a 100 MB budget for full images (oldest full images are evicted first). Screens that cannot be safely captured show **No snapshot**.

While controlling the computer, send/get clipboard text or upload/drop a file (25 MB maximum). Clipboard access requires an explicit button action. Browser permission failures expose a manual text field. Uploads use a unique name in this Mac's `Downloads/Allternit`, a Linux guest's `/tmp`, or a Windows guest's Public Downloads. The resulting path appears after transfer. Focus the viewer and use **⌘/Ctrl + Shift + H** to toggle take-over and hand-back. Phone and PWA share these controls.

### Runtime routes

All routes below use the existing authenticated computer visibility rules:

| Route | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/v1/computers/:id/history` | Current action and chronological steps grouped by `run_id`, thumbnails, retention bounds |
| `GET /api/v1/computers/:id/history/:step/snapshot` | Recorded redacted PNG, private/no-store; validates step UUID |
| `GET /api/v1/computers/:id/clipboard` | UTF-8 clipboard text, capped at 256 KB |
| `PUT /api/v1/computers/:id/clipboard` | Explicit human clipboard write `{text}` |
| `GET /api/v1/computers/:id/transfer/file` | Requires control; returns supported file and chunk limits |
| `POST /api/v1/computers/:id/transfer/file?name=…&upload=…&offset=…&size=…` | Sequential chunks up to 1 MB, 25 MB per file; final acknowledgement returns the safe unique destination path |

Clipboard and file transfer require this user's active control lease on this device, or this user's active bot desktop take-over. Watching access alone cannot transfer data. Step recording hooks into both direct computer tool calls and structured subtask steps; it never bypasses action policy, lease or approvals.

File uploads use 1 MB chunks so they fit through the phone runtime relay. The runtime checks ownership and active control on every chunk and isolates partial uploads by user, computer, and upload ID. Up to four partial uploads are retained in memory; entries older than ten minutes are pruned on the next upload. No destination file is written until all bytes arrive in order.

## Online, asleep or offline

Settings → Computers shows each paired computer as **Online**, **Asleep** or **Offline**, when it was last seen, and the next routine it is due to run. The same list appears in Settings on the phone layout and the installed phone app.

Your computer tells the cloud it is awake every minute. If it goes quiet, Allternit keeps watching for you, because the cloud is awake when your Mac is not:

- A routine came due more than 10 minutes ago and your computer didn't run it. You get an alert such as "Morning briefing didn't run: your computer is asleep."
- Texts or other channel messages have waited more than 10 minutes for your computer. You get "3 texts are waiting for your computer."
- A message could not be delivered for 24 hours and was dropped. This is a critical alert, never a silent drop.

Each alert closes by itself when your computer comes back. If the status can't be loaded, the list shows the computer's last known status instead of guessing.
