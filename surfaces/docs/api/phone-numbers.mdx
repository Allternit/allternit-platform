---
title: Phone numbers and SMS
description: Give a bot a real phone number, receive texts as a thread, send replies, and the consent rules that apply.
---

A bot can have a real phone number. People text it, the text lands in a thread, and the bot's reply goes back as an SMS. The cloud API owns the number, the carrier, texting compliance, and the consent record. The user's runtime (a cloud computer or Desktop) owns the conversation.

All routes are on `api.allternit.com` and take a Clerk session or a `compute`-scoped API key, like the channel address routes. When the carrier is not configured they answer `503 {"error":"phone_not_configured"}`.

## Turn it on (operators)

| Env var | What |
|---|---|
| `ALLTERNIT_PHONE_CARRIER` | `telnyx` (default) or `twilio` |
| `ALLTERNIT_TELNYX_API_KEY`, `ALLTERNIT_TELNYX_PUBLIC_KEY` | Telnyx API key, and the base64 ed25519 webhook public key |
| `ALLTERNIT_TWILIO_ACCOUNT_SID`, `ALLTERNIT_TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN` | Twilio fallback |
| `ALLTERNIT_PHONE_MAX_PER_USER` | Overrides the per-plan number limit for every paid plan (defaults: Plus 3, Super 5, Ultra 10) |
| `ALLTERNIT_SMS_DAILY_CAP` | Texts per number per day (default 1000) |

Migration `024_phone_numbers.sql` is applied by hand to the production database, like `020` and `021`.

## Numbers

- `GET /api/v1/phone/numbers/search?country=US&areaCode=415&locality=&type=local|toll_free&limit=10` returns `{carrier, numbers:[{e164, type, locality, monthlyCost}]}`.
- `POST /api/v1/phone/numbers` with `{e164, runtimeId, botId}` buys the number, assigns it to the bot, and creates its relay address (provider `sms`). Returns `201 {number}`. Phone numbers need a paid plan: Free gets `402 phone_requires_plan`. Each plan holds a limited number of numbers (`PLAN_NUMBER_LIMITS` in `routes/phone.rs`: Plus 3, Super 5, Ultra 10), past which the route returns `403 number_limit`. Porting a number follows the same rules. Errors: `402 phone_requires_plan`, `409 number_taken`, `403 number_limit`, `404 runtime_not_found`.
- `POST /api/v1/phone/numbers/port` with `{e164, runtimeId, botId}` starts a port-in (Telnyx). The number starts texting through Allternit when the port completes. `GET /api/v1/phone/numbers/:id/port` returns `{port:{orderId,state}, number}`.
- `GET /api/v1/phone/numbers` lists the caller's numbers. `DELETE /api/v1/phone/numbers/:id` releases the number and revokes its relay address.

A number's `smsState` is `pending_registration`, `active`, `rejected` or `blocked`. A new US number is `pending_registration` until the carrier approves its registration.

## Registration

US carriers require registration before a number can text at volume.

- `POST /api/v1/phone/numbers/:id/registration` takes the business form (legal name, EIN, website, address, contact, use case, sample messages, optional `optInPageUrl`). Local numbers register for 10DLC, toll-free numbers for toll-free verification; `kind` overrides. The stored copy masks the EIN.
- `GET /api/v1/phone/numbers/:id/registration` returns the state (`pending`, `approved`, `rejected`, with `rejectionReason`) and the number's `smsState`. It re-reads a pending registration from the carrier. A rejected registration can be submitted again.
- The carrier **message flow** is generated, not taken from the form: one opt-in path, the person texts the number first after seeing it on the opt-in page, plus the STOP/START/HELP keywords and the terms and privacy links. Carriers reject flows that list several paths (signed forms, verbal consent) without proof for each. `optInPageUrl` is the business's own page; when it is empty the registration uses the hosted page `GET /sms/:numberId` (public HTML: the number, the use case, the texting terms; `404` for a number with no registration or a released one). A free-text `optInWorkflow` is still accepted and stored but never sent to the carrier.
- 10DLC is two steps at the carrier: the business **brand** is verified first (usually hours to a few days), then the texting **campaign** is reviewed. The submit files the brand right away; if the brand is still being verified, the campaign is filed automatically as soon as the brand clears (on the carrier's status webhook or the next `GET`). `campaignId` stays `null` until then. A campaign the carrier refuses outright turns the registration `rejected` with a `rejectionReason` starting `campaign:`. Submitting the form again for a rejected 10DLC registration whose brand cleared corrects the existing campaign in place (`PUT /10dlc/campaign/{id}` at Telnyx, which puts it back into review) and keeps the same registration: no new brand or campaign is filed. A registration rejected at the brand step files a new one.
- The campaign sent to the carrier includes the HELP, STOP and START replies (naming the business display name and contact email), and declares that messages may include links and phone numbers.
- **Waiting, not rejected.** When the carrier refuses for a reason on Allternit's side (our carrier account's balance or level, or a carrier outage), the registration stays `pending` with a `rejectionReason` starting `waiting:`, and is retried automatically every 30 minutes. Only a refusal about the business's own details turns it `rejected`.
- **Individuals (sole proprietors, no EIN).** Send `entityType: "SOLE_PROPRIETOR"`, `contactFirstName`, `contactLastName` and `mobilePhone` (instead of an EIN) and `useCase: "SOLE_PROPRIETOR"`. The carrier texts a code to that mobile; `POST /api/v1/phone/numbers/:id/registration/otp` with `{"pin":"123456"}` checks it (400 when it doesn't match) and files the campaign; an empty body sends a new code. Carriers allow one number per individual this way, with lower sending limits.

See [Phone invites](/api/phone-invites) for single-use invite links that record explicit consent.

## Texts in

The carrier posts to the number's relay address. Before anything is queued the cloud verifies the carrier signature, drops duplicates, and handles keywords:

- `STOP`, `STOPALL`, `UNSUBSCRIBE`, `CANCEL`, `END`, `QUIT`: the sender is added to the number's opt-out list, gets a confirmation, and nothing reaches the runtime.
- `HELP`: gets a help reply.
- `START`, `UNSTOP`: removes the opt-out and confirms.

An opted-out sender never reaches the runtime. Every consent change is logged. A person who texts first has consented to the bot answering; on an active number their first text gets the registered opt-in confirmation once (sender name, frequency, rates, STOP/HELP, no sharing for marketing) before the bot's reply. STOP, START and HELP replies use the registered sender name and contact email, the same strings the carrier campaign declares.

The runtime receives one normalised request at `/webhooks/channels/sms`:

```json
{ "provider": "sms", "messageId": "…", "numberId": "…", "botId": "…",
  "from": "+15551230000", "to": "+14155550101", "text": "…", "receivedAt": "…" }
```

## Texts out

`POST /api/v1/channels/sms/send` with `{numberId, to, text}` returns `{ok:true, messageId, parts}`. It refuses with:

| Status | `error` | When |
|---|---|---|
| 403 | `sms_not_active` | The number isn't registered yet |
| 403 | `recipient_opted_out` | The recipient sent STOP |
| 403 | `no_consent` | The recipient never texted the number first (START after STOP also counts). Recorded consent and inbound calls allow calls, not texts |
| 429 | `daily_limit` | Over the number's daily cap |
| 400 | `bad_request` | Not an E.164 number, empty text, or over 1,600 characters |

`POST /api/v1/phone/numbers/:id/consent` with `{e164, source, evidence}` records explicit consent you hold (for example a web form). It allows calls, not texts: the carrier campaign registers texting-first opt-in only. It never lifts a STOP; only the person's own START does.

## Outbound calls

`POST /api/v1/phone/calls/outbound` with `{numberId, to, botId, purpose}` returns `{consentRef, basis, expiresAt}` when the callee texted or called the number first, or has an explicit consent record, and hasn't opted out. Otherwise `403 {"error":"no_consent"}`. The dial itself belongs to the voice service, which presents the `consentRef`.

## Warm transfers

A warm transfer rings a third party, so the call worker refuses it unless the control carries a `consentRef`. Cloud-api attaches one when it forwards `POST /api/v1/voice/calls/{callId}/control` with `{"action":"transfer","to":"+1…","mode":"warm"}`; cold transfers need none.

| Who asked | `consentRef` basis | When |
|---|---|---|
| The signed-in owner (the default) | `owner_directed` | Always, unless the target replied STOP on this number. |
| The bot (`"initiator":"bot"`) | `owner_transfer_target` | Only if `to` is in the bot's `transferTargets`. Otherwise no ref, and the worker fails the transfer cleanly (`call.transferred` with `ok:false`). |

The reference lives 30 minutes in `call_consents` with `purpose: "transfer"`. STOP always wins over both bases.

### Transfer targets

The owner approves who a bot may transfer to: `PUT /api/v1/voice/bot-config/{botId}` with `{"transferTargets":[{"e164":"+14155550100","label":"Front desk"}]}`. The list replaces the stored one (up to 20 E.164 numbers, deduplicated; `[]` clears it; leaving the field out keeps it). The response includes `transferTargets`. Each newly approved target logs an `explicit` consent row with source `owner_transfer_target` on the bot's live numbers. Cloud-api migration 036 adds `voice_bot_config.transfer_targets`; apply it by hand to prod like 020 and 021.

The bot call settings screen in allternit-ai edits this list as "Transfer targets" (a small separate change on the app side).
