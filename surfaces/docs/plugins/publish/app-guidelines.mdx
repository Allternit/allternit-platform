---
title: 'App guidelines'
description: 'Quality, safety, privacy, and honesty expectations for apps in the Allternit directory'
---

These guidelines describe what reviewers look for. Some are checked by the [automated scan](/plugins/publish/submit-and-review#2-automated-scan). Others are judged by a person. Following them does not guarantee approval.

## Do something useful and finish the job

- Solve a specific problem. An app should do something a user would ask for in a sentence.
- Make each tool do one thing and describe it precisely. Prefer several clear tools over one tool with a mode parameter.
- Return results the model can use: concise text in `content`, typed data in `structuredContent`. Report failures with `isError: true` and a message that says what to try next.
- Keep responses fast and small. A View should show something useful before every request has finished.
- Test on light and dark themes, at narrow widths, and in fullscreen.

## Describe your app honestly

- The name, descriptions, icon, and screenshots must show what the app does today. Do not describe features you have not built.
- Do not claim endorsement or affiliation you do not have. Do not imitate Allternit or another company's interface.
- Declare tool annotations that match behavior. A tool that writes, deletes, or sends must not be marked `readOnlyHint: true`. See [annotations](/plugins/develop/mcp-server#annotations).
- Provide a real privacy policy and terms of service at public `https` URLs.
- Do not use Allternit's brand, or a person's name, likeness, or voice, without permission.

## Do not steer the model

Tool descriptions and server instructions are for explaining your tools. They are not for competing with other apps or overriding the model's instructions. The scan flags descriptions that:

- tell the model to always or only use your tool,
- tell it not to use other tools or apps, or to use yours instead of them,
- call your tool the best or preferred option,
- try to override earlier or system instructions, or contain injected instruction markup,
- force your tool to run before any other.

Say what your tool does and when it is the right choice. Leave the decision to the model and the user.

## Minimize the data you ask for and keep

- Request only the scopes and tool inputs you need to do the task. Do not add inputs "for later".
- Do not ask for, or infer, sensitive information the task does not need: government identifiers, precise location, health data, financial account numbers, or credentials.
- Do not put secrets, tokens, or conversation-wide context into tool results or View state.
- Keep retention short and tell users what you keep in your privacy policy. Delete data on request.
- Send only the data a tool call needs to your own backend and to third parties you name.

## Keep users in control

- **Ask before consequential actions.** Anything that spends money, sends a message, or deletes data should be a separate, clearly named tool with `destructiveHint` or `openWorldHint` set, so Allternit can prompt.
- **Use permissions sparingly.** Declare `camera`, `microphone`, `geolocation`, or `clipboardWrite` only if the feature needs it, and explain why in the listing.
- **Keep the View's network access narrow.** List exact hosts in `connectDomains` and `resourceDomains`. Avoid wildcards, non-TLS origins, and `frameDomains`. If you need `frameDomains`, be ready to justify each one.
- **Do not fight the sandbox.** Do not try to read the host page, other Views, or other users' data, or to escape the iframe.

## Sell honestly

If you charge for anything, follow [Selling in your MCP App](/plugins/guides/selling): a real refund policy, accurate line items, and no hidden charges. Commerce is a preview and test mode only.

## Security

- Validate every token: signature, issuer, expiry, and audience. See [Authentication](/plugins/develop/authentication).
- Treat tool inputs as untrusted. Validate them on the server, whether the call came from the model or from a View.
- Do not put credentials in `mcp.json` or in the package.
- Keep your server patched, and tell your users if you have a data incident.

## What reviewers may reject

- Apps that do not work with the test account or the test cases you supplied.
- Tool metadata that does not match behavior.
- Descriptions that steer the model.
- Requests for more data or more permissions than the task needs.
- Misleading listings, missing policies, or impersonation.
- Apps that put users, their data, or their accounts at risk.
